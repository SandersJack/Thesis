    %% Intro with background on NA48/2
    The analysis aims to set limits on the mixing parameter of the \gls{DP} utilising the production of $A'$ through the decay of $\pi^0 \to \gamma A'$ and its subsequent decay into SM particles $A' \to e^+e^-$. The theoretical background for this hypothetical \gls{DP} is described in Section \ref{sec:DarkP_th}. 
    The NA62 experiment, described in Section \ref{sec:na62} provides a clean sample of $\pi^0$ particles through the decay of $K^+ \to \pi^+\pi^0$ with a branching ratio of 20.67\,\%. With the assumption that the \gls{DP} decays promptly, where the $A'$ decays at the point of its production, the decay signature is identical to the Dalitz decay 
    $\pi^0_D \to e^+e^-$. This decay represents the background for the \gls{DP} search and determines the experiment's sensitivity.

    \section{Event Selection}
    %The analysis started using the same procedure used in the NA48/2 \cite{2015178}
    The analysis strategy for the \gls{DP} search originated from the selection used for the search at NA48/2 \cite{2015178}. As the \gls{kpid} decay has the same event signature as the \gls{DP} the selection strategy involves the selection of the $\pi^+$ and an electron-positron pair. The selection strategy is set out below:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Require that at least one of the following triggers is satisfied: the Control Trigger (also referred to as the Minimum Bias Trigger), the Multi-Track (MT) trigger, or the Electron Multi-Track (eMT) trigger, described in Chapter~\ref{sec:na62}.
        \item A vertex is selected if the total momentum is below \SI{78}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight}, a total positive charge of 1, a $\chi^{2}$ less than 40 and a vertex position between 105\, and 180\,m.
        \item The tracks within the vertex are checked if they are in acceptance of all four Straw spectrometer chambers and the CHOD. The track momentum must be greater than \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight} and less than \SI{60}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight} with a $\chi^+$ of less than 30. All three tracks must traverse the first chamber of the straw with a separation of  20\,cm from each other.
        \item Require the presence of a three-track in the vertex with a selected positively charged pion ($\pi^+$) and a selected electron-positron pair ($e^{\pm}$). The pion is selected from the two positively charged tracks using the missing mass between the kaon momentum. The kaon momentum is taken from the average beam momentum. 
        If the missing mass of the kaon and a positive track is within five sigma of the $\pi^0$ mass hypothesis, it is selected as a charged pion. If both positive tracks satisfy the condition, the event is vetoed. From \gls{MC}, only approximately $0.1\,\%$ of Dalitz decays have both positive tracks satisfying the selection of the charged pion.
        The electron is assigned to the only negative track. The positive track which doesn't pass the charged pion selection is selected as the positron.
        \item A selected photon is also required. The missing momentum of the vertex is calculated with the kaon momentum and the total vertex momentum. It was used to extrapolate a straight line from the vertex position to the z plane of the LKr calorimeter. Within a radius of 15\,cm of the extrapolated position, a single cluster
        with a minimum energy of \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt} is selected as a photon from the decay. The photon energy must be within \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt} of the calculated missing vertex momentum.
        \item An elliptical cut is introduced to reduce the background that would otherwise pass the selection. The reconstructed mass of the electron, positron and photon is compared against the reconstructed mass of the electron, positron, photon and charged pion.
    \end{itemize}
    The major difference between the selection used for the NA62 compared to the NA48/2 study is the \gls{PID}. The study at NA48/2 used calorimetric \gls{PID} where the \gls{LKr} was utilised to reconstruct the energy of the pion and the electron-positron pair. The reconstructed energy over momentum was used to distinguish 
    between the electron/positron and pion. Whereas in the NA62 search described in the selection above, the \gls{PID} utilised the momentum measurements to reconstruct the invariant masses, which could then be compared against the known PDG masses. This change was driven by the difference in experimental setup between NA48/2 and NA62. The NA62 setup is longer, being designed for much higher momenta 
    which results in the \gls{LKr} being further away from the decay point, reducing the acceptance of the \gls{kpid} decay at NA62 compared to NA48/2. The \gls{LKr} was still used in the analysis to reconstruct the photon energy, which was required to prevent other backgrounds from passing the selection.

    The equation used for the elliptical cut in the selection is defined as,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:elipse}
        \frac{((x - x_c)cos(\theta) + (y-y_c)sin(\theta))^2}{a^2} + \frac{((y - y_c)cos(\theta) - (x-x_c)sin(\theta))^2}{b^2} <= 1
    \end{equation}
    where $x_c$ and $y_c$ are the ellipse's centre in the x and y dimensions. The angle $\theta$ is the rotation of the ellipse and $a$, $b$ are the length of the semi-major and the semi-minor axis, respectively. 
    The points $x$ and $y$ are the reconstructed mass values of the candidate event. The event is selected if the equation returns a value that is less than or equal to 1. The results from Figure \ref{fig:reconMasses} and Equations \ref{eq:piee_sig} \& \ref{eq:gammaee_sig}was used as starting point for the parameter values. These were
    optimised for a tight cut with an acceptable number of events passing. The optimisation produced parameters for the ellipse defined below, 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $(x_c = 493.37)$: The x-coordinate of the ellipse's center.
        \item $(y_c = 134.89)$: The y-coordinate of the ellipse's center.
        \item $(a = 10.24)$: The semi-major axis of the ellipse ($4 \times \sigma_{\pi ee \gamma}$).
        \item $(b = 4.29)$: The semi-minor axis of the ellipse ($3 \times \sigma_{ee \gamma}$).
        \item $(\theta = 0.4887)$: The rotation angle of the ellipse in radians.
        \item $((x, y))$: The point being tested for inclusion within the ellipse.
    \end{itemize}
    The size of the semi-major and minor axis was chosen to maximise the amount of $\pi^0_D$ decays passing the selection and to have the ellipse as small as possible. This was required to reduce the chance of other backgrounds from passing selection.
    When utilised in Equation \ref{eq:elipse}, the parameters described above produced an ellipse shown in red in Figure \ref{fig:elip}. All the events from the \gls{kpid} \gls{MC} sample reaching the end of the selection are shown in Figure \ref{fig:elip}. 
    It shows that the ellipse is as tight as possible to collect the majority of the \gls{kpid} events.

    \section{Dark Photon Search}
    Two methods were employed in the background estimation for the Dark Photon Search. The first method utilised generated \gls{MC} samples of the expected background decays as the background estimate. However, it produced unsatisfactory results. This led to the development of a second method which used a data-driven method. 
    Both methods are described below. 
    \subsection{\gls{MC} Background Method}
    %As stated previously, the \gls{MC} background method only utilised the 2022 NA62 dataset. 
    The limited size of the \gls{MC} samples was expected to impact the final result when used for the background estimate. However, due to compute time and file size, it was not feasible to directly increase the statistics on the main samples. Still, using a feature of the framework, it was possible to increase the statistics across a portion of the reconstructed mass spectra.
    The true invariant mass of the electron positron pair can be used to calculate the kinematic variable $x$ from Equation \ref{eq:XParam}. 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:XParam}
        x = \frac{(Q_{e^-} + Q_{e^+})^2}{m_{\pi^0}^2} = \frac{m_{ee}^2}{m_{\pi^0}^2}
    \end{equation}
    where $Q_{e^-}$ and $Q_{e^+}$ are the four-momentum of the electron and position pair, and $m_{\pi^0}$ is the mass of the neutral pion.
    The resultant distribution of the \gls{kpid} in terms of the the kinematic variable $x$ from the \gls{MC} is shown in Figure \ref{fig:trueMC_X}. The decay width in Equation \ref{eq:partDecay} strongly depends on the kinematic term $x$, reducing to zero as the reconstructed invariant mass of the electron-position pair reached the $\pi^0$ mass. The na62 software framework allows for the simulation of the Dalitz decay only when the kinematic variable $x > 0.5$. From the true \gls{MC} distribution in 
    Figure \ref{fig:trueMC_X} the fraction of events above 0.5 was 0.003. Producing a simulation with the cut on the kinematic variable $x$ with a fraction of the size of the main sample, would decrease the errors on the \gls{MC} counts by orders of magnitude. 
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{plots/TrueMC_X.png}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Reconstructed missing mass of electron-positron pair of in terms of reconstructed kinematic variable $x$}
        \label{fig:trueMC_X}
    \end{figure}
    For the rest of the study, the following \gls{MC} samples were used
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 200\,M $K \to \pi^0_D \pi^+ $ ($x < 0.5$)
        \item 5\,M $K \to \pi^0_D \pi^+ $ ($x > 0.5$)
        \item 50\,M $K \to \pi^0_D \mu^+ \nu_{\mu}$ 
    \end{itemize}
    To improve the combination of the two \gls{kpid} samples, events where $x > 0.5$ were removed from the main sample at the selection stage. 
    After the full selection of the \gls{MC} samples they were combined into a single dataset to describe the background. Using the known $N_K$, computed in Section \ref{sec:kaonFlux} for the 2022 dataset the \gls{MC} 
    was scaled to match the number of events in the data. This resulted in the background estimation in terms of the electron positron invariant mass shown in Figure \ref{fig:mee_mc}.
    % the reconstructed mass of the electron-positron pair is shown in Figure \ref{fig:mee_mc}. Using the computed $N_K$, the \gls{MC} was scaled to match the data.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{plots/mee_mc_new.pdf}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Reconstructed mass of electron-positron pair of data and \gls{MC} samples.}
        \label{fig:mee_mc}
    \end{figure}
    The simulation of the $\pi^0_D$ used the lowest-order differential decay rate \cite{PhysRevD.5.1763} defined below,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:partDecay}
        \frac{d^2\Gamma}{dxdy} = \Gamma_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi}|F(x)|^2\frac{(1-x)^3}{4x} \left(1+y^2+\frac{r^2}{x}\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $\Gamma_0$ is the $\pi^0 \to \gamma \gamma$ differential decay rate, $\alpha$ is the fine-structure constant, F(x) is the pion \gls{TFF} and x,y are the kinematic variables defined in Equation \ref{eq:XParam} and below,
    \begin{equation}
        y = \frac{2Q_{\pi^+}(Q_{e^-} - Q_{e^+})}{m_{\pi^0}^2 (1-x)}
    \end{equation}
    The variables $Q_{e^-}$, $Q_{e^+}$ and $Q_{\pi^+}$ are the four-momentum of the electron, positron and charged pion. Within the kinematic region of Dalitz decay, the \gls{TFF} is expected to vary slowly as x increases and can be approximated as $F(x) = 1 + ax$. The variable $a$ is the slope factor, which has been set through theory and a number of experiments. 
    The Vector Meson Dominance (VMD) model expects that $a = m_{\pi^0} (m_{\rho}^2 + m_{\omega}^2) / 2 = 0.03$ \cite{PhysRevD.83.037503}.
    Whereas the \gls{PDG} average for all experiments was $a = 0.0335 \pm 0.0031$ \cite{Tanabashi:2018oca}. However, due to large errors and inconsistent results experimentally, it is not possible to rely on external values for the slope factor for the MC.  
    In the NA62 framework, by default, the slope factor is defined as $a =  0.032$. 
    
    The comparison in Figure \ref{fig:MCDATAFF} shows data over \gls{MC} from Figure \ref{fig:mee_mc} in terms of the kinematic parameter $x$ described in Equation \ref{eq:XParam}. This distribution suggests that there was a discrepancy between the expected and data as $x$ increases. The dependence along $x$ suggests that the TFF found in the framework is incompatible for use in the background estimate with this data set.
    As the \gls{MC} samples had already been produced, a correction was required to remove the applied \gls{TFF}. %The \gls{MC} is re-weighted using the events passing the selection compared against the expected number of events from \gls{MC} across the reconstructed mass range.

        \centering

    \begin{equation} \label{eq:TFFsq}
        F(x)^2 = (1 + ax)^2
    \end{equation}
    The fitted line shown in Figure \ref{fig:MCDATAFF} is the \gls{TFF} of the framework which was removed from the data by inverting the effects of Equation \ref{eq:TFFsq}. This resulted in the distribution shown in Figure \ref{fig:MCDATANOFF}. The data over \gls{MC} with the removed effects of the \gls{TFF} was fitted with equation \ref{eq:TFFsq} using a least $\chi^2$ method, with the resultant TFF shown as a line in Figure \ref{fig:MCDATANOFF}. The fitted TFF in Figure \ref{fig:MCDATANOFF} shows a slope factor of,
    \begin{equation}
        a = 0.0210 \pm 0.0029.
    \end{equation}

    %Now talk about procedure
    The range of the \gls{DP} search is defined as $9 \leq m_{A'} \leq 120$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight^2}. The lower mass boundary is defined due to the falling geometric acceptance of the decay and the low electron identification efficiency at low momentum. The upper boundary is defined due to both the falling geometrical acceptance and suppressed decay width. 
    To select the search window centers and size of the windows, the reconstructed mass resolution was considered. The reconstructed mass resolution was calculated using MC, where the difference between the true mass and the reconstructed mass of the electron-positron pair was collected for each mass bin and shown in Figure \ref{fig:true_reco}.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{plots/true_reco.png}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{True invariant mass minus reconstructed invariant mass of the electron-positron pair in terms of true invariant mass.}
        \label{fig:true_reco}
    \end{figure}
    For each invariant mass bin, the Y projection is taken, and a Gaussian fit is conducted. The sigma of the fit is recorded and used to produce Figure \ref{fig:mass_res}. The resultant points in blue are fitted to a straight line, shown in orange representing an equation for the reconstructed invariant mass resolution, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:mass_res}
        \sigma_m(m_{ee}) = \SI{0.03755}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight^2} + 0.00554 \times m_{ee}.  
    \end{equation}
    The NA62 resolution is a significant improvement compared to NA48/2, shown as red line in Figure \ref{fig:mass_res} due to improved detector design. The improvement in mass resolution allows for a smaller search mass window compared to the previous study at NA48/2.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{plots/mass_res.png}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Sigma of the difference between true and reconstructed invariant mass of the electron-positron pair in bin of true invariant mass.}
        \label{fig:mass_res}
    \end{figure}
    The window centres are separated by $3\sigma_m(m_{A'})$, with a window width of $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(m_{A'})$ rounded to the nearest 0.01\,\si{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight^2}. The rounding was required as this is the size of the output bins from the selection of the invariant mass spectrum. This results in 177 \gls{DP} mass values tested.

    For each \gls{DP} mass value, the number of data events ($N_{obs}$) is compared to the number of expected events obtained from \gls{MC} ($N_{exp}$). The $N_{obs}$, $N_{exp}$ and their subsequent errors are shown in Figure \ref{fig:searchwindow_vals}. The error on the number of data events $\delta N_{obs}$ resulted from the statistical error from the number of 
    events within each bin $\delta N_{obs} = \sqrt{N_{obs}}$. The error on the expected number of events is due to the statistical error on the number of \gls{MC} events within the search window.  A correction is required due to the scale factor imposed on the \gls{MC} to equate for the correct effective kaon flux, defined in Section \ref{sec:kaonFlux}. The error for $N_{exp}$ is defined as 
    $\delta N_{exp} = sf \times \sqrt{N_{exp} / sf}$ where $sf$ was the scale factor used to scale the \gls{MC} sample. It would have been required to include statistical errors due to the trigger efficiencies, but as mentioned previously, all \gls{MC} samples employed a trigger emulator, so this was not required.

    For each \gls{DP} mass window the local significance of the \gls{DP} signal was calculated, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:localSig}
        Z = \frac{N_{obs} - N_{exp}}{\sqrt{\delta N_{obs} + \delta N_{exp}}}
    \end{equation}
    and is shown in Figure \ref{fig:local_sig}. Across the search window, the local significance is never greater than $3 \sigma$, resulting in no \gls{DP} signal observed. 
    The \gls{UL} for the number of \gls{DP} decays ($A' \to e^+e^-$) at a 90\% \gls{CL} for each \gls{DP} mass value is calculated with $N_{obs}$, $N_{exp}$ and $\delta N_{exp}$ using the CLs method. This method is recommended by the Particle Data Group \cite{Cowan:2018} and employs the process set out in \cite{Junk:1999kv, Read:2000ru}.
    For setting an \gls{UL} on the signal $s$ from a counting experiment where $N$ events are observed, and there is a known expected background $b$, it can be considered a hypothesis test between the two cases: signal+background and just background. According to the Neyman-Pearson lemma \cite{NeymanPearson1933}, the most powerful test statistic for the hypothesis is the likelihood ratio, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:test_stat}
        X = L_{s+b} / L_b = \frac{e^{-s+b}(s+b)^N}{N!} / \frac{e^{-b} b^N}{N!} = e^{-s}(1+\frac{s}{b})^N.
    \end{equation} 
    It is beneficial to denote the test statistic for the observed data as $X_{obs}$.
    A confidence level for the exclusion of the background hypothesis ($\text{CL}_{s+b}$) is the probability that the test statistic defined in Equation \ref{eq:test_stat} is less than or equal than the observed test statistic, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:prob_test}
        CL_{s+b} = P_{s+b}(X \leq X_{obs}).
    \end{equation}
    To improve the computational efficiency, the test statistic defined in Equation \ref{eq:test_stat} used for comparison is simplified to, 
    \begin{equation}
        log(X) = N log(\frac{s}{b}).
    \end{equation}
    The confidence level is calculated using a Monte-Carlo integration method where a large number of trials are used to calculate the probability defined in Equation \ref{eq:prob_test}. For each trial, the value N is sampled from a Poisson distribution with a mean of $s+b$ where b is the background sampled from a normal distribution with a mean equal to the expected number of events and sigma equal to the error on the expected value.
    The test statistic is computed and compared with $X_{obs}$. Similarly, the confidence level for the exclusion of the signal+background ($\text{CL}_{b}$) is calculated, with the difference being that N is sampled from a Poisson distribution with a mean of $b$ instead of $s+b$. 
    After the trials the modified frequentist confidence level ($\text{CL}_s$) is calculated as,
    \begin{equation}
        CL_s = CL_{s+b} / CL_b.
    \end{equation}
    A binary search is then completed using the above method to find the value of $s$, which satisfies the relation, 
    \begin{equation}
        CL_s(s) = 1 - \alpha
    \end{equation}
    which would be the \gls{UL} on the signal for a confidence level of $\alpha$.

    \subsection{Data-Driven Background Method}
    The data-driven background method employed the same selection described previously. Instead of using the produced \gls{MC} samples for the background estimation, the data itself was used. In addition to the 2022 dataset used in the \gls{MC} background study, the 2023 and 2024 datasets were included for this study with the number of kaon decays 
    defined in Section \ref{sec:kaonFlux}. The resultant reconstructed invariant mass of the electron-positron pair for events passing the selection is shown in Figure \ref{fig:22_23_data}.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{plots/22_23_24_data.pdf}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Reconstructed invariant mass of electron-positron pair for events passing the selection from the 2022 and 2023 NA62 datasets.}
        \label{fig:22_23_data}
    \end{figure}
    The distribution of the data itself is used to estimate the background. The distribution is fitted using polynomials and the side bands of the data, using the process described below.

    
    A method similar to the previous section using the reconstructed mass resolution, defined with equation \ref{eq:mass_res}, is used to convert bins of $M_{ee}$ to \gls{DP} candidate windows.
    A new \gls{DP} mass range is defined as $6 \leq m_{A'} \leq 121.6$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}. The mass ranges were extended compared to the previous \gls{MC} approach due to the large number of events in the mass bins at the extreme ends of the distribution afforded by only using the data sample rather than the MC. Each \gls{DP} mass hypothesis is defined by steps of $1\sigma_m(m_{A'})$. 
    This deviated from the \gls{MC} study where the window's width was the size of the mass centre step. Now, the mass windows overlap, increasing the number of mass hypotheses.
    A 4th-order polynomial is fitted to the data distribution using a least $\chi^2$ method. The fitting region for each mass hypothesis is defined as $\pm 15\sigma_m(m_{A'})$ from the mass hypothesis, and the distribution is re-binned for each point with a bin width of $1/5\sigma_m(m_{A'})$. 
    However, data events in the mass bins above \SI{125}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared} were removed due to the small counts causing instability with the fitting.
    Events in the mass bins $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(m_{A'})$
    of the mass hypothesis were excluded from the fit to avoid the possibility of the presence of a \gls{DP} signal, which would result in a biased background estimate.
    The number of expected events $N_{exp}$ is evaluated as the integral of the fitted functions across the $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(m_{A'})$ mass window divided by the resultant bin width. The error on the number of expected events $\delta N_{exp}$ is a combination of statistical and systematic uncertainties. The statistical component $\delta N_{exp}^{stat}$ is computed from the propagation of the 
    statistical errors of the polynomial parameters, provided in the form of a covariance matrix and is calculated using an error propagation method available in the ROOT package \cite{Brun1997} (TF1::IntegralError()). 
    The systematic error $\delta N_{exp}^{sys}$ is estimated by fitting each distribution defined above with alternative polynomials, in this case, 3rd and 5th order. The resultant integrals were evaluated and compared to the integral calculated using the main polynomial to produce the systematic error.

    The lowest limit on the mixing parameter $\epsilon^2$ occurs at low \gls{DP} mass due to the limited kinematic suppression of the \gls{DP} decay. Key to this analysis was the assumption of the prompt decay, where the \gls{DP} decays instantly into the lepton-antilepton pair. To ensure that the calculated limits above are correct, a check on the \gls{DP} max mean path length was required. 
    To calculate the max mean path of the \gls{DP}, the max energy of the \gls{DP} was required. From a nominal beam energy at NA62 of \SI{75}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight} the maximum energy of the $\pi^0$ from the $K_{2\pi}$ decay was calculated in the kaons rest reference frame,
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:2body}
        E_{\pi^0}^{rest} = \frac{M^2 + m_{\pi^0}^2 - m_{\pi^+}^2}{2M} = \SI{0.246}{\giga\electronvolt}
    \end{equation}
    where $M$, $m_{\pi^0}$ and $m_{\pi^+}$ were the kaon, neutral pion and charged pion masses, with the equation derived from basic two body decay kinematics. The $\pi^0$ momentum in the kaon rest frame was also defined as
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:pi0mom}
        p_{\pi^0}^{rest} = \sqrt{(E_{\pi^0}^{rest})^2 - m_{\pi^0}^2} 
    \end{equation}
    The Equations \ref{eq:2body} and \ref{eq:pi0mom} are used to calculate the maximum energy of the $\pi^0$ in the laboratory reference frame via a Lorentz boost
    \begin{equation}
        E^{lab}_{\pi^0} = \gamma_K (E_{\pi^0}^{rest} + \beta p_{\pi^0}^{rest}) = \SI{68.5}{\giga\electronvolt}
    \end{equation}
    with $\gamma_K = E_{K} / M$. In the decay $\pi^0 \to \gamma A'$ the maximum energy of the \gls{DP} would occur when all the energy of the neutral pion is transferred to the \gls{DP} and the photon is at rest, resulting in a max \gls{DP} energy of $E^{lab}_{A'} = \SI{68.5}{\giga\electronvolt}$. 
    The max \gls{DP} mean free path is defined as, 
    \begin{equation}
        \lambda_{max} = \gamma_{max}c\tau_{A'} = \frac{E_{max}}{m_{A'} c^2} c \tau_{A'}
    \end{equation}
    where \gls{DP} mean free path is inversely dependent on $\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2$. The lowest limit from the analysis is found as 
    $\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2 = 3.36 \times 10^{-6}$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt^2\per\clight^4}  %$\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2 = 6.80 \times 10^{-6} MeV^2 /c^4$ 
    corresponding to a max \gls{DP} mean path length of $1.629$\,m. %$0.81\m$.
    Due to the construction of the 3-track trigger and offline reconstruction, the resolution of a decay vertex position is around 1\,m. With a vertex greater than 1\,m, a three-track vertex will be created by the reconstruction, which will result in the event being removed due to the requirement of one three-track vertex, reducing the acceptance.
    However, this is only true in the most extreme cases where all the energy is propagated from the kaon to the \gls{DP}. In this extreme case, it is possible to visualise the 1\,m prompt limit, which is shown in Figure \ref{fig:prompt} as the dashed lines. The two lines represent the 1\,m limit for the maximum \gls{DP} energy and 50\,\% of the maximum. The fluctuations 
    of the UP on the mixing parameter fall between these two lines, suggesting that the prompt assumption is still valid except in the most extreme cases.

    \section{Conclusions}
    The search for \gls{DP} at NA62 has continued and expanded from the study conducted at NA48/2. Two methods for the background estimation have been presented: one utilising \gls{MC} samples and the other utilising the data itself.
    It was found that using the \gls{MC} background estimation method produced uncompetitive results due to the limited size of the samples produced. The data driven background estimation however provided excellent results producing competitive limits for the mixing parameter across the 
    \gls{DP} mass search range. The result covers an area which is yet to be covered by any other experiment at the time of writing. A final result will be available when NA62 completes data taking in 2026 and the full dataset is used for the analysis.
