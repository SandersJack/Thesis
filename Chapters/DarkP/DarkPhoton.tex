    %% Intro with background on NA48/2
    The analysis aims to set limits on the mixing parameter of the \gls{DP} utilising the production of $A'$ through the decay of $\pi^0 \to \gamma A'$ and its subsequent decay into SM particles $A' \to e^+e^-$. The theoretical background for this hypothetical \gls{DP} is set out in Section \ref{sec:DarkP_th}. 
    The NA62 experiment, which is described in Section \ref{sec:na62} provides a clean sample of $\pi^0$ through the decay of $K^+ \to \pi^+\pi^0$ which has a branching ratio of 20.67\,\%. With the assumption that the \gls{DP} decays promptly, where the $A'$ decays at the point of its production, the decay signature is identical to the Dalitz decay 
    $\pi^0_D \to e^+e^-$. This decay represents the background for the \gls{DP} search and determines the experiment's sensitivity.

    \section{Event Selection}
    %The analysis started using the same procedure used in the NA48/2 \cite{2015178}
    The analysis strategy for the \gls{DP} search first originated from the selection used for the search at NA48/2 \cite{2015178}. The event selection strategy used in the search for the \gls{DP} followed the selection of the Dalitz decay:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Require either the Control Trigger (sometimes described as Minimum Bias Trigger), Multi-Track (MT) or Electron Multi Track (eMT), which are described in Chapter \ref{sec:na62}
        \item A vertex was selected if the total momentum was below \SI{78}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight}, a total positive charge of 1, a $\chi^{2}$ less than 40 and a vertex position between 105\, and 180\,m.
        \item The tracks associated with the vertex are selected if they are in acceptance of all four Straw spectrometer chambers and the CHOD. The track momentum must be greater than \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight} and less than \SI{60}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight} with a $\Chi^+$ of less than 30. The tracks must traverse within 20\,cm of each other.
        \item Require the presence of a three-track in the vertex with a selected positively charged pion ($\pi^+$) and a selected electron-positron pair ($\pi^{\pm}$). The pion is selected using the missing mass between the Kaon momentum, which is taken from the average beam momentum and the two positive tracks. 
        If the positive track is within five sigma of the $\pi^0$ mass hypothesis, it is selected as a charged pion. If both positive tracks satisfy the condition, the event is vetoed. From MC, only approximately $0.1/,\%$ of Dalitz decays have both positive tracks satisfying the selection of the charged pion.
        The electron was assigned to the only negative track and the negative track which doesn't pass the charged pion selection was selected as the positron.
        \item A selected photon is also required. The missing momentum of the vertex was calculated with the Kaon momentum and the total vertex momentum. It was used to extrapolate a straight line from the vertex position to the z plane of the LKr calorimeter. Within a radius of 15\,cm of the extrapolated position, a single cluster
        with a minimum energy of \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt} is selected as a photon from the decay. The photon energy has to satisfy the condition of being within \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt} of the calculated missing vertex momentum.
        \item An elliptical cut reduces background that would otherwise pass the selection. The reconstructed mass of the electron, positron and photon is compared against the reconstructed mass of the electron, positron, photon and charged pion.
    \end{itemize}
    The major difference between the selection used for the NA62 study and NA48/2 is the particle identification strategy. The study at NA48/2 utilised calorimetric \gls{PID} where the \gls{LKr} was utilised to reconstruct the energy of the pion and the electron-positron pair. The reconstructed energy over momentum was then used to distinguish 
    between the electron/positron and pion. Whereas in the NA62 search described in the selection above, the \gls{PID} utilised the momentum measurements to reconstruct the invariant masses, which could then be compared against the known PDG masses. This change was driven by the difference in experimental setup between NA48/2 and NA62, where the latter is much longer as it's designed for much higher momenta. 
    This results in the \gls{LKr} being further away from the decay point, which reduces the acceptance of the decay at NA62 compared to NA48/2. The \gls{LKr} was still used in the analysis to reconstruct the photon energy, which was required to prevent other backgrounds from passing the selection.

    The equation used for the elliptical cut in the selection is defined below,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:elipse}
        \frac{((x - x_c)cos(\theta) + (y-y_c)sin(\theta))^2}{a^2} + \frac{((y - y_c)cos(\theta) - (x-x_c)sin(\theta))^2}{a^2} <= 1
    \end{equation}
    where $x_c$ and $y_c$ are the ellipse's centre in the x and y dimensions. $\theta$ being the rotation of the ellipse, $a$ and $b$ are the length of the semi-major and the semi-minor axis, respectively. 
    The points $x$ and $y$ are the reconstructed mass values of the candidate event. The event is selected if the equation returns a value that is less than or equal to 1, which is the definition of an ellipse. Starting with the results from Figure \ref{fig:reconMasses} and Equations \ref{eq:piee_sig} \& \ref{eq:gammaee_sig}, the parameters were
    optimised for a tight cut with an acceptable number of events passing. The optimisation produced parameters for the ellipse defined below, 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $(x_c = 493.37)$: The x-coordinate of the ellipse's center.
        \item $(y_c = 134.89)$: The y-coordinate of the ellipse's center.
        \item $(a = 10.24)$: The semi-major axis of the ellipse ($4 \times \sigma_M_{\pi ee \gamma}$).
        \item $(b = 4.29)$: The semi-minor axis of the ellipse ($3 \times \sigma_M_{ee \gamma}$).
        \item $(\theta = 0.4887)$: The rotation angle of the ellipse in radians.
        \item $((x, y))$: The point being tested for inclusion within the ellipse.
    \end{itemize}
    The size of the semi-major and minor axis was chosen to maximise the amount of $\pi^0_D$ decays passing the selection and also to have the ellipse as small as possible to reduce the chance of other backgrounds being introduced in the selected events.
    When inputted into Equation \ref{eq:elipse}, the parameters described above produced an ellipse shown in red in Figure \ref{fig:elip}. Figure \ref{fig:elip} shows all the events from the \gls{kpid} MC sample reaching the end of the selection just before the elliptical cut. 
    It shows that the ellipse is as tight as possible to collect the majority of the \gls{kpid} events.

    \section{Dark Photon Search}
    Two methods were employed in the background estimation for the Dark Photon Search. The first method generated MC samples of the expected background as the background estimate. However, it produced unsatisfactory results, which led to the second method, which used a data-driven method. 
    Both methods are described below. 
    \subsection{MC Background Method}
    As stated previously, the MC background method only utilised the 2022 NA62 dataset. 
    The limited size of the MC samples was expected to impact the final result when used for the background estimate. However, due to compute time and file size, it was not feasible to directly increase the statistics on the main samples. However, using a feature of the framework, it was possible to increase the statistics across a portion of the reconstructed mass spectra.
    The true invariant mass of the electron positron pair can be used to calculate the kinematic variable $x$ from Equation \ref{eq:XParam}. 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:XParam}
        x = \frac{(Q_{e^-} + Q_{e^+})^2}{m_{\pi^0}^2} = \frac{m_{ee}^2}{m_{\pi^0}^2}
    \end{equation}
    The resultant distribution from the MC sample is shown in Figure \ref{fig:trueMC_X}. The decay width in Equation \ref{eq:partDecay} strongly depends on the kinematic term $x$, reducing to zero as the reconstructed invariant mass reached the $\pi^0$ mass. The Framework allows for the simulation of the Dalitz decay where the kinematic variable $x > 0.5$. From the true MC distribution in 
    Figure \ref{fig:trueMC_X} the fraction of events above 0.5 was 0.003. Producing a simulation with the enabled, even with a fraction of the size of the main sample, would decrease the errors on the MC counts by orders of magnitude. 
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{plots/TrueMC_X.png}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Reconstructed missing mass of electron-positron pair of in terms of reconstructed kinematic variable $x$}
        \label{fig:trueMC_X}
    \end{figure}
    For the rest of the study, the following MC samples were used
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 200\,M $K \to \pi^0_D \pi^+ $ ($x < 0.5$)
        \item 5\,M $K \to \pi^0_D \pi^+ $ ($x > 0.5$)
        \item 50\,M $K \to \pi^0_D \mu^+ \nu_{\mu}$ 
    \end{itemize}
    To improve the combination of the two $K_{2\pi_D}$ samples, the large sample had events where $x > 0.5$ were removed at the selection stage. 
    After the full selection of the MC samples and data, the reconstructed mass of the electron-positron pair is shown in Figure \ref{fig:mee_mc}. Using the computed $N_K$, the MC was scaled to match the data.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{plots/mee_mc.png}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Reconstructed mass of electron-positron pair of data and MC samples.}
        \label{fig:mee_mc}

    The samples used include the simulation of the $\pi^0_D$ using the lowest-order differential decay rate \cite{PhysRevD.5.1763} and is defined below
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:partDecay}
        \frac{d^2\Gamma}{dxdy} = \Gamma_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi}|F(x)|^2\frac{(1-x)^3}{4x} \left(1+y^2+\frac{r^2}{x}\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $\Gamma_0$ is the $\pi^0 \to \gamma \gamma$ differential decay rate, $\alpha$ is the fine-structure constant, F(x) is the pion \gls{TFF} and x,y are kinematic variables defined below
    \begin{equation}
        y = \frac{2Q_{\pi^+}(Q_{e^-} - Q_{e^+})}{m_{\pi^0}^2 (1-x)}
    \end{equation}
    where $Q_{e^-}$, $Q_{e^+}$ and $Q_{\pi^+}$ are the four-momentum of the electron, position and charged pion. Within the kinematic region of Dalitz decay, the \gld{TFF} is expected to vary slowly as x increases and can be approximated as $F(x) = 1 + ax$, with $a$ being the slope factor, which has been set through theory and experimentally. 
    The Vector Meson Dominance (VMD) model expects that $a = m_{\pi^0} (m_{\rho}^2 + m_{\omega}^2) / 2 = 0.03$ \cite{PhysRevD.83.037503}.
    Experimentally, the \gls{PDG} average for all experiments was $a = 0.0335± 0.0031$ \cite{Tanabashi:2018oca}. However, due to large errors and inconsistent results experimentally and through theory, it is not possible to rely on external values for the slope factor for the MC.  
    In the NA62 framework, by default, the slope factor is defined as $a =  0.032$. The MC samples have already been produced, a correction was made to remove the applied \gls{TFF}. The data is re-weighted using the events passing the selection compared against the expected number of events from MC across the reconstructed mass range.
    This comparison in Figure \ref{fig:MCDATAFF} shows the data over MC from Figure \ref{fig:mee_mc} in terms of the kinematic parameter $x$ described in Equation \ref{eq:XParam}. This distribution suggests that there is a discrepancy between the expected and data, especially as $x$ increases. The dependence along $x$ suggests that the TFF found in the framework is unacceptable for use in the background estimate with this data set.

The fitted TFF in Figure \ref{fig:MCDATANOFF} represents a slope factor of,

    The range of the \gls{DP} search was defined as $9 \leq m_{A'} \leq 120$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight^2}. The lower mass boundary is defined due to the falling geometric acceptance of the decay and the low electron identification efficiency at low momentum. The upper boundary was also defined due to both the falling geometrical acceptance and suppressed decay width. 
    To select the search window centers and the size of the windows, the reconstructed mass resolution was considered. The reconstructed mass resolution was calculated using MC, where the difference between the true mass and the reconstructed mass of the electron-positron pair was collected for each mass bin and is shown in Figure \ref{fig:true_reco}.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{plots/true_reco.png}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{True invariant mass minus reconstructed invariant mass of the electron-positron pair in terms of true invariant mass.}
        \label{fig:true_reco}
    \end{figure}
    For each invariant mass bin, the Y projection is taken, and a Gaussian fit is conducted. The sigma of the fit is recorded and used to produce the plot shown in Figure \ref{fig:mass_res}. The resultant points in blue are fitted to a straight line shown in orange, producing an equation for the reconstructed invariant mass resolution, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:mass_res}
        \sigma_m(m_{ee}) = \SI{0.03755}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight^2} + 0.00554 \times m_{ee}.  
    \end{equation}
    The NA62 resolution is a significant improvement compared to NA48/2, shown as red line in Figure \ref{fig:mass_res} due to improved detector design. The improvement in mass resolution allows for a smaller search mass window compared to the previous study at NA48/2.
    \begin{figure}[H]

    Using Equation \ref{eq:mass_res}, the window centres are separated by $3\sigma_m(m_{A'})$, with a window width of $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(m_{A'})$ rounded to the nearest 0.01\,\si{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight^2}. The rounding was implemented as this was the size of the saved bins of the invariant mass spectrum, resulting in 177 mass values tested.

    For each \gls{DP} mass value, the number of data events ($N_{obs}$) was compared to the number of expected events obtained from MC ($N_{exp}$). The $N_{obs}$, $N_{exp}$ and their subsequent errors are shown in Figure \ref{fig:searchwindow_vals}. The error on the number of data events $\delta N_{obs}$ resulted from the statistical error from the number of 
    events within each bin $\delta N_{obs} = \sqrt{N_{obs}}$. The error on the expected number of events was due to the statistical error on the number of MC events within the search window. This, however had to be corrected for the scale factor imposed on the MC to equate for the correct effective kaon flux, defined in Section \ref{sec:kaonFlux}, resulting in 
    $\delta N_{exp} = sf \times \sqrt{N_{exp} / sf}$ where $sf$ was the scale factor used to scale the MC sample. It would have been required to include statistical errors due to the trigger efficiencies, but as mentioned previously, all MC samples employed a trigger emulator, so this was not required.

    For each \gls{DP} mass window the local significance of the \gls{DP} signal was calculated, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:localSig}
        Z = \frac{N_{obs} - N_{exp}}{\sqrt{\delta N_{obs} + \delta N_{exp}}}
    \end{equation}
    and is shown in Figure \ref{fig:local_sig}. Across the search window, the local significance is never greater than $3 \sigma$, resulting in no \gls{DP} signal observed. 
    The \gls{UL} for the number of \gls{DP} decays ($A' \to e^+e^-$) at a 90\% \gls{CL} for each \gls{DP} mass value was calculated using $N_{obs}$, $N_{exp}$ and $\delta N_{exp}$ using the CLs method recommended by the Particle Data Group \cite{Cowan:2018} and employs the process set out in \cite{Junk:1999kv, Read:2000ru}.
    For setting an \gls{UL} on the signal $s$ from a counting experiment where $N$ events are observed, and there is a known expected background $b$, it can be considered a hypothesis test between the two cases: signal+background and just background. According to the Neyman-Pearson lemma \cite{NeymanPearson1933}, the most powerful test statistic for the hypothesis is the likelihood ratio, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:test_stat}
        X = L_{s+b} / L_b = \frac{e^{-s+b}(s+b)^N}{N!} / \frac{e^{-b} b^N}{N!} = e^{-s}(1+\frac{s}{b})^N.
    \end{equation} 
    It is beneficial to denote the test statistic for the observed data as $X_{obs}$.
    A confidence level for the exclusion of the background hypothesis ($\text{CL}_{s+b}$) is the probability that the test statistic defined in Equation \ref{eq:test_stat} is less than or equal than the observed test statistic, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:prob_test}
        CL_{s+b} = P_{s+b}(X \leq X_{obs}).
    \end{equation}
    To improve the computational efficiency, the test statistic defined in Equation \ref{eq:test_stat} used for comparison is simplified to, 
    \begin{equation}
        log(X) = N log(\frac{s}{b}).
    \end{equation}
    The confidence level is calculated using a Monte-Carlo integration method where a large number of trials are used to calculate the probability defined in Equation \ref{eq:prob_test}. For each trial, the value N is sampled from the Poisson distribution with a mean of s+b, with b being the background sampled from a normal distribution with a mean equal to the expected number of events and sigma equal to the error on the expected value.
    The test statistic is computed and compared with $X_{obs}$. Similarly, the confidence level for the exclusion of the signal+background ($\text{CL}_{b}$) is calculated, with the difference being that N is sampled from a Poisson distribution with a mean of $b$ compared to $s+b$. 
    After the trials the modified frequentist confidence level ($\text{CL}_s$) is calculated as,
    \begin{equation}
        CL_s = CL_{s+b} / CL_b.
    \end{equation}
    A binary search is then completed using the above method to find the value of $s$, which satisfies the relation, 
    \begin{equation}
        CL_s(s) = 1 - \alpha
    \end{equation}
    which would be the \gls{UL} on the signal for a confidence level of $\alpha$.

    \subsection{Data Driven Background Method}
    The data-driven background method employed the same selection described previously. Instead of using the produced MC samples for the background estimation, the data itself was used. In addition to the 2022 dataset used in the MC background study, the 2023 and 2024 datasets were included for this study with the number of kaon decays 
    defined in Section \ref{sec:kaonFlux}. The resultant reconstructed invariant mass of the electron-positron pair for events passing the selection is Shown in Figure \ref{fig:22_23_data}.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{plots/22_23_24_data.pdf}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Reconstructed invariant mass of electron-positron pair for events passing the selection from the 2022 and 2023 NA62 datasets.}
        \label{fig:22_23_data}
    \end{figure}
    The distribution of the data itself was used to estimate the background. The distribution was fitted using polynomials and the side bands of the data, using the process described below.

    
    A method similar to the previous section using the reconstructed mass resolution, defined with equation \ref{eq:mass_res}, was used to convert bins of $M_{ee}$ to \gls{DP} candidate windows.
    A new \gls{DP} mass range was defined as $6 \leq m_{A'} \leq 121.6$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}. The mass ranges were extended compared to the previous MC approach due to the large number of events in the mass bins at the extreme ends of the distribution afforded by only using the data sample rather than the MC. Each \gls{DP} mass hypothesis was defined by steps of $1\sigma_m(m_{A'})$. 
    This is different from the MC study where the window's width was the size of the mass centre step. Now, the mass windows overlap dramatically, increasing the number of mass hypotheses.
    A 4th-order polynomial was fitted to the data distribution using a least $\chi^2$ method. The fitting region for each mass hypothesis was defined as $\pm 15\sigma_m(m_{A'})$ from the mass hypothesis, and the distribution was re-binned for each point with a bin width of $1/5\sigma_m(m_{A'})$. 
    However, data events in the mass bins above \SI{125}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared} were removed due to the small counts causing instability with the fitting.
    Counts in the mass bins $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(m_{A'})$
    of the mass hypothesis were excluded from the fit to avoid the possibility of sensitivity reduction due to the presence of a \gls{DP} signal, which would result in a biased background estimate.
    The number of expected events $N_{exp}$ was evaluated as the integral of the fitted functions across the $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(m_{A'})$ mass window divided by the resultant bin width. The error on the number of expected events $\delta N_{exp}$ was a combination of statistical and systematic. The statistical $\delta N_{exp}^{stat}$ was computed from the propagation of the 
    statistical errors of the polynomial parameters, provided in the form of a covariance matrix and was calculated using an error propagation method available in the ROOT package \cite{Brun1997} (TF1::IntegralError()). 
    The systematic error $\delta N_{exp}^{sys}$ was estimated by fitting each distribution defined above with alternative polynomials, in this case, 3rd and 5th order. The resultant integrals were evaluated and compared to the integral calculated using the main polynomial.

    The lowest limit on the mixing parameter $\epsilon^2$ occurs at low \gls{DP} mass due to the limited kinematic suppression of the \gls{DP} decay. Key to this analysis was the assumption of the prompt decay, where the \gls{DP} decays instantly into the lepton-antilepton pair. To ensure that the calculated limits above are correct, a check on the \gls{DP} max mean path length was required. 
    To calculate the max mean path of the \gls{DP}, the max energy of the \gls{DP} was required. From a nominal beam energy at NA62 of \SI{75}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight} the maximum energy of the $\pi^0$ from the $K_{2\pi}$ decay was calculated in the kaons rest reference frame,
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:2body}
        E_{\pi^0}^{rest} = \frac{M^2 + m_{\pi^0}^2 - m_{\pi^+}^2}{2M} = \SI{0.246}{\giga\electronvolt}
    \end{equation}
    where $M$, $m_{\pi^0}$ and $m_{\pi^+}$ were the kaon, neutral pion and charged pion masses, with the equation derived from basic two body decay kinematics. The $\pi^0$ momentum in the kaon rest frame was also defined as
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:pi0mom}
        p_{\pi^0}^{rest} = \sqrt{(E_{\pi^0}^{rest})^2 - m_{\pi^0}^2} 
    \end{equation}
    The Equations \ref{eq:2body} and \ref{eq:2body} are used to calculate the maximum energy of the $\pi^0$ in the laboratory reference frame via a Lorentz boost
    \begin{equation}
        E^{lab}_{\pi^0} = \gamma_K (E_{\pi^0}^{rest} + \beta p_{\pi^0}^{rest}) = \SI{68.5}{\giga\electronvolt}
    \end{equation}
    with $\gamma_K = E_{K} / M$. In the decay $\pi^0 \to \gamma A'$ the maximum energy of the \gls{DP} would occur when all the energy of the neutral pion was transferred to the \gls{DP} and the photon was at rest, resulting in a max \gls{DP} energy of $E^{lab}_{A'} = \SI{68.5}{\giga\electronvolt}$. 
    The max \gls{DP} mean free path was defined, 
    \begin{equation}
        \lambda_{max} = \gamma_{max}c\tau_{A'} = \frac{E_{max}}{m_{A'} c^2} c \tau_{A'}
    \end{equation}
    where \gls{DP} mean free path is inversely dependent on $\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2$. The lowest limit from the analysis was found as 
    $\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2 = 3.36 \times 10^{-6}$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt^2\per\clight^4}  %$\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2 = 6.80 \times 10^{-6} MeV^2 /c^4$ 
    corresponding to a max \gls{DP} mean path length of $1.629\,m$.%$0.81\m$. 
    Due to the construction of the 3-track trigger and offline reconstruction, the resolution of a decay vertex position is around 1\,m. With a vertex greater than 1\,m, a three-track vertex will be created by the reconstruction, which will result in the event being removed due to the requirement of one three-track vertex, reducing the acceptance. 
    However, this is only true in the most extreme cases where all the energy is propagated from the kaon to the \gls{DP}. In this extreme case, it is possible to visualise the 1\,m prompt limit, which is shown in Figure \ref{fig:prompt} as the dashed lines. The two lines represent the 1\,m limit for the maximum \gls{DP} energy and 50\,\% of the maximum. The fluctuations 
    of the UP on the mixing parameter fall between these two lines, suggesting that the prompt assumption is still valid except in the most extreme cases.
