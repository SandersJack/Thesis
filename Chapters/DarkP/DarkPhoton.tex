    %% Intro with background on NA48/2
    This chapter summarises the search for the \gls{DP} at NA62, called A', with the data collected from 2022 to 2024.
    The analysis aims to set limits on the mixing parameter of the \gls{DP} utilising the production of $A'$ through the decay of $\pi^0 \to \gamma A'$ and its subsequent decay into SM particles $A' \to e^+e^-$. The theoretical background for this hypothetical \gls{DP} is described in Section \ref{sec:DarkP_th}. 
    The NA62 experiment, described in Section \ref{sec:na62}, provides a clean sample of $\pi^0$ particles through the decay of \mbox{$K^+ \to \pi^+\pi^0$} with a branching ratio of 20.67\,\%. With the assumption that the \gls{DP} decays promptly, where the $A'$ decays at the point of its production, the decay signature is identical to the Dalitz decay 
    $\pi^0_D \to \gamma e^+e^-$. This decay represents the background for the \gls{DP} search and determines the experiment's sensitivity.

    \section{\boldmath $\pi^0_D$ \unboldmath Event Selection}
    %The analysis started using the same procedure used in the NA48/2 \cite{2015178} %ADD na48 context
    The analysis strategy for the \gls{DP} search originated from the selection used for the search at NA48/2 \cite{2015178}. As the \gls{kpid} decay has the same event signature as the production and decay of the \gls{DP} and involves the selection of the $\pi^+$ and an electron-positron pair. The selection is set out below:
    \subsection{Vertex Selection}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Require that at least one of the following trigger conditions is satisfied: the Control Trigger (also referred to as the Minimum Bias Trigger), the Multi-Track (MT) trigger, or the Electron Multi-Track (eMT) trigger, described in Chapter~\ref{sec:na62}.
        \item A three-track vertex is selected if the total momentum is below \SI{78}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight}, a total positive charge of $Q=1$, a $\chi^{2} < 40$ and a vertex position $\SI{105}{\meter} < Z_{vtx} \SI{180}{\meter}$.
        \item The tracks within the vertex are checked if they are in acceptance of all four Straw spectrometer chambers and the CHOD. The track momentum must be $\SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight} < p < \SI{60}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight}$ with a $\chi^2 < 30$. All three tracks must traverse the first STRAW chamber with a separation of 20\,mm from each other.
        \item A selected photon is also required. The missing momentum of the vertex is calculated with the kaon momentum and the total vertex momentum. It is used to extrapolate a straight line from the vertex position to the z-plane of the LKr calorimeter. Within a radius of 15\,cm of the extrapolated position, a single cluster
        with a minimum energy of \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt} is selected as a photon from the decay. The photon energy must be within \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt} of the calculated missing vertex momentum.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsection{Kinematic PID}    
    The presence of a three-track vertex is required with a positively charged pion ($\pi^+$) and an electron-positron pair ($e^{\pm}$). The pion is identified from the two positively charged tracks using the missing mass between the kaon momentum and the track momentum, with the kaon momentum taken from the average beam momentum. 
    If the missing mass of the kaon and the positive track is consistent with the $\pi^0$ mass hypothesis, it is identified as a charged pion. 
    The cut on the reconstructed missing mass of the kaon and charged pion system is calculated using a \gls{kpid} \gls{MC} sample and is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:kaonpion}.
    The width of the distribution is used for selection of the charged pion and is taken from a fitted Gaussian on the distribution in Figure~\ref{fig:kaonpion} and calculated as
    \begin{equation}
        \sigma = 3.984 \pm 0.007_{Stat}\, \SI{}{\mega\electronvolt}.
    \end{equation} 
    \begin{figure}[t]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{plots/kaonpion.pdf}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Reconstructed missing mass of kaon and charged pion compared to the known neutral pion mass fitted with a Gaussian.}
        \label{fig:kaonpion}
    \end{figure}
    If both positive tracks satisfy the condition, the event is vetoed. From \gls{MC}, only approximately $0.1\,\%$ of Dalitz decays have both positive tracks satisfying the selection of the charged pion.
    The electron is assigned to the only negative track. The positive track which doesn't pass the charged pion selection is selected as the positron.
    
    %An elliptical cut is introduced to reduce the background that would otherwise pass the selection. The reconstructed mass of the electron, positron and photon is compared against the reconstructed mass of the electron, positron, photon and charged pion.
    An elliptical cut conducted on the final reconstructed masses $M_{ee \gamma}$ and $M_{\pi ee \gamma}$ and is defined using a \gls{kpid} \gls{MC} sample. The reconstructed masses are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:reconMasses}. 
    The widths of the reconstructed masses $M_{ee \gamma}$ and $M_{\pi ee \gamma}$ are calculated by fitting the distributions shown in Figure~\ref{fig:reconMasses} with Gaussian distributions.
    The calculated sigma for the reconstructed mass $M_{\pi ee \gamma}$ is calculated as
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:piee_sig}
        \sigma_{\pi ee \gamma} = 2.47 \pm 0.01_{Stat}\,\SI{}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared},
    \end{equation}
    and for $M_{ee \gamma}$, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:gammaee_sig}
        \sigma_{ee \gamma} = 1.33 \pm 0.01_{Stat}\,\SI{}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared},
    \end{equation} 
    which are used as an origin for the elliptical parameters.
    \begin{figure}[t]
        \centering
        \subfloat{{\includegraphics[height=8cm, width=0.5\textwidth]{plots/Mpeeg.png} }}%
        \subfloat{{\includegraphics[height=8cm, width=0.5\textwidth]{plots/Meeg.png} }}%
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Reconstructed masses of the decay systems. Left: the total of charged pion, electron, positron, and photon. Right:  the electron, positron, and photon.}%
        \label{fig:reconMasses}%
    \end{figure}
    The equation used for the elliptical cut in the selection is defined as
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:elipse}
        \frac{((x - x_c)\cos{\theta} + (y-y_c)\sin{\theta})^2}{a^2} + \frac{((y - y_c)\cos{\theta} - (x-x_c)\sin{\theta})^2}{b^2} \leq 1
    \end{equation}
    where $x_c$ and $y_c$ are the ellipse centre in the x and y directions. The angle $\theta$ is the rotation of the ellipse, and $a$ and $b$ are the lengths of the semi-major and the semi-minor axis, respectively. 
    The points $x$ and $y$ are the reconstructed mass values of the candidate event. The results from Figure~\ref{fig:reconMasses} and Equations \ref{eq:piee_sig} \& \ref{eq:gammaee_sig} are used as starting points for the parameter values. 
    The parameters are optimised to produce a selection efficiency of at least 90\,\% while keeping the area of the ellipse as small as possible. The parameters for the ellipse are defined below: 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $x_c = \SI{493.37}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}$: The x-coordinate of the ellipse centre.
        \item $y_c = \SI{134.89}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}$: The y-coordinate of the ellipse centre.
        \item $a = \SI{10.24}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}$: The semi-major axis of the ellipse ($4 \times \sigma_{\pi ee \gamma}$).
        \item $b = \SI{4.29}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}$: The semi-minor axis of the ellipse ($3 \times \sigma_{ee \gamma}$).
        \item $\theta = \SI{0.4887}{\radian}$: The rotation angle of the ellipse in radians.
        \item $(x, y)$: The point being tested for inclusion within the ellipse.
    \end{itemize}
    When utilised in Equation~\ref{eq:elipse}, the parameters described above produce an ellipse shown in red in Figure~\ref{fig:elip}.

    \subsection{Selection Acceptance}
    The acceptance of the \gls{kpid} \gls{MC} sample is defined across the reconstructed electron-positron mass in the range $2m_e < M_{ee} < m_{\pi^0}$ and is compared against the acceptance of the NA48/2 analysis \cite{2015178} in Figure~\ref{fig:accept}.
   % Introducing the kinematic selection compared to the calorimeter PID improved the overall acceptance. 
    The difference between the selection used for the NA62 study and the NA48/2 is the \gls{PID}. The study at NA48/2 uses calorimetric \gls{PID} where the \gls{LKr} is utilised to reconstruct the energy of the pion and the electron-positron pair. The reconstructed energy to momentum ratio is used to distinguish 
    between the electron/positron and pion. In the NA62 study, described in the selection above, the \gls{PID} uses the track momentum measurements to reconstruct the invariant masses, which is then compared against the known PDG masses. This approach is driven by the difference in experimental setup between NA48/2 and NA62. The NA62 setup is longer, as it requires enhanced \gls{PID}, satisfied by the \gls{RICH} and improved momentum measurements provided by the longer spectrometer.
    This results in the \gls{LKr} being further away from the decay volume, reducing the acceptance of the \gls{kpid} decay at NA62 compared to NA48/2. The \gls{LKr} is used in the analysis to reconstruct the photon energy.%, which is required to prevent other backgrounds from passing the selection. 
    This is apparent at low momenta where NA48/2 has an acceptance advantage.

    \section{\gls{MC} Background Estimation Method}
    %As stated previously, the \gls{MC} background method only utilised the 2022 NA62 dataset. 
    As described above the background for the search for \gls{DP} is a combination of \gls{kpid} and \gls{kmu3D}. These decays are modelled by \gls{MC} and compared against the data to determine the sensitivity of the search. The \gls{MC} samples are produced using the NA62 framework, which simulates the full detector response and the trigger system. The \gls{kpid} and \gls{kmu3D} are produced with 200 and 50 million events, respectively.
    The limited size of the \gls{MC} samples will impact the final result when used for the background estimate. However, due to compute time and file size, it is not feasible to directly increase the statistics on the main samples. Utilising a feature of the NA62 framework, the statistics across a portion of the reconstructed mass range are increased.
    The true \gls{MC} invariant mass of the electron-positron pair is used to calculate the kinematic variable $x$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:XParam}
        x = \frac{(Q_{e^-} + Q_{e^+})^2}{m_{\pi^0}^2} = \frac{m_{ee}^2}{m_{\pi^0}^2}
    \end{equation}
    where $Q_{e^-}$ and $Q_{e^+}$ are the four-momenta of the electron and positron pair, and $m_{\pi^0}$ is the mass of the neutral pion.
    The resultant distribution of the \gls{kpid} in terms of the kinematic variable $x$ from the \gls{MC} is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:trueMC_X}. The decay width, defined in Equation~\ref{eq:partDecay}, strongly depends on the kinematic term $x$. The decay width reduces to zero as the invariant mass of the electron-positron pair reaches the $\pi^0$ mass. The NA62 software framework allows for the simulation of the Dalitz decay only when the kinematic variable $x > 0.5$. From the true \gls{MC} distribution in 
    Figure~\ref{fig:trueMC_X} the fraction of events where $x > 0.5$ is 0.003. Producing a simulation with the cut on the kinematic variable $x$ with a fraction of the size of the main sample decreases the errors on the \gls{MC} counts by orders of magnitude. 
    \begin{figure}[t]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{plots/TrueMC_X.png}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{True invariant mass of the electron-positron pair divided by the $\pi^0$ mass of the \gls{kpid} \gls{MC} sample, which is equal to the kinematic variable $x$.}
        \label{fig:trueMC_X}
    \end{figure}
    For the rest of the study, the following \gls{MC} samples are used,
    \begin{itemize}
        \item 200\,M $K \to \pi^0_D \pi^+ $ ($x < 0.5$) (Main);
        \item 5\,M $K \to \pi^0_D \pi^+ $ ($x > 0.5$);
        \item 50\,M $K \to \pi^0_D \mu^+ \nu_{\mu}$.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsection{The $\pi^0$ Transition Form Factor}
    The simulation of the $\pi^0_D$ uses the lowest-order differential decay rate \cite{PhysRevD.5.1763} defined below,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:partDecay}
        \frac{d^2\Gamma}{dxdy} = \Gamma_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi}|F(x)|^2\frac{(1-x)^3}{4x} \left(1+y^2+\frac{r^2}{x}\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $\Gamma_0$ is the $\pi^0 \to \gamma \gamma$ differential decay rate, $\alpha$ is the fine-structure constant, F(x) is the pion \gls{TFF}, $x$ and $y$ are the kinematic variables defined in Equation~\ref{eq:XParam} and below,
    \begin{equation}
        y = \frac{2Q_{\pi^+}(Q_{e^-} - Q_{e^+})}{m_{\pi^0}^2 (1-x)}.
    \end{equation}
    The variables $Q_{e^-}$, $Q_{e^+}$ and $Q_{\pi^+}$ are the four-momenta of the electron, positron and charged pion. Within the kinematic region of Dalitz decay, the \gls{TFF} varies slowly as x increases and is approximated as $F(x) = 1 + ax$. The variable $a$ is the slope factor, which has been set through theoretical predictions and a number of experiments. 
    The Vector Meson Dominance (VMD) model expects that \mbox{$a = m_{\pi^0} (m_{\rho}^2 + m_{\omega}^2) / 2 = 0.03$ \cite{PhysRevD.83.037503}}.
    For experiments, the \gls{PDG} average for all experiments is \mbox{$a = 0.0335 \pm 0.0031$} \cite{Tanabashi:2018oca}. However due to significant errors and inconsistent results experimentally, it is not possible to rely on external values for the slope factor for the MC.  
    In the NA62 framework, the slope factor is defined as $a =  0.032$ by default. 
    
    The comparison in Figure~\ref{fig:MCDATAFF} shows the data over \gls{MC} from Figure~\ref{fig:mee_mc} in terms of the kinematic parameter $x$ described in Equation~\ref{eq:XParam}. This distribution shows a discrepancy between the expected background and data as $x$ increases. The dependence along $x$ suggests that the TFF found in the framework is incompatible with the background estimate for this data set.
    As the \gls{MC} samples have already been produced, a correction is required to remove the applied \gls{TFF}. %The \gls{MC} is re-weighted using the events passing the selection compared against the expected number of events from \gls{MC} across the reconstructed mass range.
    \begin{figure}[t]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{plots/MCDataWithFF.png}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Data/\gls{MC} in terms of the reconstructed kinematic variable $x$ with the framework \gls{TFF}. The red line shows the fit of the \gls{TFF} equation, described by Equation~\ref{eq:TFFsq}.}
        \label{fig:MCDATAFF}
    \end{figure}
    The \gls{TFF} used by the framework is removed by fitting the square of the TFF,
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:TFFsq}
        F(x)^2 = (1 + ax)^2
    \end{equation}
    on the data over \gls{MC} distribution.
    The fitted line shown in Figure~\ref{fig:MCDATAFF} is the \gls{TFF} of the framework, which is removed from the data by inverting the effects of Equation~\ref{eq:TFFsq}. This results in the distribution shown in Figure~\ref{fig:MCDATANOFF}. The data over \gls{MC} with the removed \gls{TFF} is fitted with Equation~\ref{eq:TFFsq} using a least $\chi^2$ method, with the resultant TFF shown as a line in Figure~\ref{fig:MCDATANOFF}. The fitted TFF in Figure~\ref{fig:MCDATANOFF} produces a slope factor of,
    \begin{equation}
        a = 0.021 \pm 0.003.
    \end{equation}

    %Now talk about procedure
    The range of the \gls{DP} search is defined as $9 \leq m_{A'} \leq 120$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight^2}. The lower mass boundary is defined due to the falling geometric acceptance of the decay and the low electron identification efficiency at low momentum. The upper boundary is defined due to both the falling geometrical acceptance and suppressed decay width.

    \subsection{Upper Limit on the Mixing Parameter}
    For each \gls{DP} mass value, the number of data events ($N_{obs}$) is compared to the number of expected events obtained from \gls{MC} ($N_{exp}$). The $N_{obs}$, $N_{exp}$ and their subsequent errors are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:searchwindow_vals}. The error on the number of data events $\delta N_{obs}$ is calculated from the statistical error from the number of 
    events within each bin $\delta N_{obs} = \sqrt{N_{obs}}$. The error on the expected number of events is due to the statistical error of \gls{MC} events within the search window.  A correction is required due to the scale factor imposed on the \gls{MC} to equate for the correct effective kaon flux, defined in Section \ref{sec:kaonFlux}. The error for $N_{exp}$ is defined as 
    $\delta N_{exp} = sf \times \sqrt{N_{exp} / sf}$ where $sf$ is the scale factor used to scale the \gls{MC} sample. It would have been required to include statistical errors due to the trigger efficiencies. However, as mentioned previously, all \gls{MC} samples employed a trigger emulator, so this is not required.

        and the resultant \gls{UL} on the number of Dark Photon candidates to a 90\% confidence level.}
        \label{fig:searchwindow_vals}
    \end{figure}
    For each \gls{DP} mass window, the local significance of the \gls{DP} signal is calculated as,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:localSig}
        Z = \frac{N_{obs} - N_{exp}}{\sqrt{\delta N_{obs} + \delta N_{exp}}}
    \end{equation}
    and is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:local_sig}. Across the search window, the local significance is never greater than $3 \sigma$, resulting in no \gls{DP} signal being observed. 
    The \gls{UL} for the number of \gls{DP} decays ($A' \to e^+e^-$) at a 90\% \gls{CL} for each \gls{DP} mass value is calculated with $N_{obs}$, $N_{exp}$ and $\delta N_{exp}$ using the CLs method. This method is recommended by the Particle Data Group \cite{Cowan:2018} and employs the process set out in \cite{Junk:1999kv, Read:2000ru}.
    For setting an \gls{UL} on the signal $s$ from a counting experiment where $N$ events are observed, and there is a known expected background $b$, it is considered a hypothesis test between the two cases: signal+background and just background. According to the Neyman-Pearson lemma \cite{NeymanPearson1933}, the most powerful test statistic for the hypothesis is the likelihood ratio, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:test_stat}
        X = L_{s+b} / L_b = \frac{e^{-s+b}(s+b)^N}{N!} / \frac{e^{-b} b^N}{N!} = e^{-s}(1+\frac{s}{b})^N.
    \end{equation} 
    It is beneficial to denote the test statistic for the observed data as $X_{obs}$.
    A confidence level for the exclusion of the background hypothesis ($\text{CL}_{s+b}$) is the probability that the test statistic defined in Equation~\ref{eq:test_stat} is less than or equal to the observed test statistic, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:prob_test}
        CL_{s+b} = P_{s+b}(X \leq X_{obs}).
    \end{equation}
    To improve the computational efficiency, the test statistic defined in Equation~\ref{eq:test_stat} used for comparison is simplified to, 
    \begin{equation}
        log(X) = N log(\frac{s}{b}).
    \end{equation}
    The \gls{CL} is calculated using a \gls{MC} integration method where a large number of trials are used to calculate the probability defined in Equation~\ref{eq:prob_test}. For each trial, the value N is sampled from a Poisson distribution with a mean of $s+b$ where b is the background sampled from a normal distribution with a mean equal to the expected number of events and sigma equal to the error on the expected value.
    The test statistic is computed and compared with $X_{obs}$. Similarly, the confidence level is also calculated for the exclusion of the signal+background ($\text{CL}_{b}$) is calculated, with the difference being that N is sampled from a Poisson distribution with a mean of $b$ instead of $s+b$. 
    After the trials the modified frequentist confidence level ($\text{CL}_s$) is calculated as,
    \begin{equation}
        CL_s = CL_{s+b} / CL_b.
    \end{equation}
    A binary search is then completed using the above method to find the value of $s$, which satisfies the relation, 
    \begin{equation}
        CL_s(s) = 1 - \alpha
    \end{equation}
    which would be the \gls{UL} on the signal for a confidence level of $\alpha$.
    The resultant \gls{UL} on the number of \gls{DP} candidates ($N_{DP}$) at a 90\% \gls{CL} is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:searchwindow_vals}.

    \subsection{Fitting the Data Distribution}
    A method similar to the previous section using the reconstructed mass resolution, defined with Equation~\ref{eq:mass_res}, is used to convert bins of $M_{ee}$ to \gls{DP} candidate windows.
    A new \gls{DP} mass range is defined as $6 \leq m_{A'} \leq 121.6$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}. The mass ranges are extended compared to the previous \gls{MC} study. This is due to the large number of events in the mass bins at the extreme ends of the distribution afforded by only using the data sample rather than the MC. Each \gls{DP} mass hypothesis is defined by steps of $1\sigma_m(m_{A'})$, resulting in 428 \gls{DP} mass hypotheses tested. 
    %This deviated from the \gls{MC} study where the window's width is the size of the mass centre step. 
    %This is  compared to the \gls{MC} study. %How many?
    A 4th-order polynomial is fitted to the data distribution using a least $\chi^2$ method. The fitting region for each mass hypothesis is defined as $\pm 15\sigma_m(m_{A'})$ from the mass hypothesis, and the distribution is re-binned with a bin width of $1/5\sigma_m(m_{A'})$. 
    Data events in the mass bins above \SI{125}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared} are removed. This is because the small counts at the edge of the mass range cause instability with the fitting.
    Events in the mass bins $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(m_{A'})$
    of the mass hypothesis are excluded from the fit to avoid the possibility of the presence of a \gls{DP} signal, which would result in a biased background estimate.
    The number of expected events $N_{exp}$ is evaluated as the integral of the fitted functions across the $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(m_{A'})$ mass window divided by the resultant bin width. The error on the number of expected events $\delta N_{exp}$ combines statistical and systematic uncertainties. The statistical component $\delta N_{exp}^{stat}$ is computed from the propagation of the 
    statistical errors of the polynomial parameters, provided in the form of a covariance matrix and is calculated using an error propagation method available in the ROOT package \cite{Brun1997} (TF1::IntegralError()). 
    The systematic error $\delta N_{exp}^{sys}$ is estimated by fitting each distribution defined above with alternative polynomials, in this case, 3rd and 5th order. The resultant integrals are evaluated and compared to the integral calculated using the main polynomial to produce the systematic error.

    \section{Prompt Limit Calculation}\label{sec:PromptLim}
    The lowest limit on the mixing parameter $\epsilon^2$ occurs at low \gls{DP} mass due to the limited kinematic suppression of the \gls{DP} decay. Key to this analysis is the assumption of the prompt decay, where the \gls{DP} decays instantly into the lepton-antilepton pair. A check on the \gls{DP} max mean path length is required to ensure that the calculated limits above are correct. 
    Using the max energy of the \gls{DP}, the max mean path of the \gls{DP} is calculated. From a nominal beam energy at NA62 of \SI{75}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight} the maximum energy of the $\pi^0$ from the $K_{2\pi}$ decay is calculated in the kaons rest reference frame,
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:2body}
        E_{\pi^0}^{rest} = \frac{M^2 + m_{\pi^0}^2 - m_{\pi^+}^2}{2M} = \SI{0.246}{\giga\electronvolt}
    \end{equation}
    where $M$, $m_{\pi^0}$ and $m_{\pi^+}$ are the kaon, neutral pion and charged pion masses, with the equation derived from basic two-body decay kinematics. The $\pi^0$ momentum in the kaon rest frame is defined as
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:pi0mom}
        p_{\pi^0}^{rest} = \sqrt{(E_{\pi^0}^{rest})^2 - m_{\pi^0}^2}.
    \end{equation}
    The Equations \ref{eq:2body} and \ref{eq:pi0mom} are used to calculate the maximum energy of the $\pi^0$ in the laboratory reference frame via a Lorentz boost,
    \begin{equation}
        E^{lab}_{\pi^0} = \gamma_K (E_{\pi^0}^{rest} + \beta p_{\pi^0}^{rest}) = \SI{68.5}{\giga\electronvolt},
    \end{equation}
    with $\gamma_K = E_{K} / M$. In the decay $\pi^0 \to \gamma A'$ the maximum energy of the \gls{DP} would occur when all the energy of the neutral pion is transferred to the \gls{DP} and the photon is at rest, resulting in a max \gls{DP} energy of $E^{lab}_{A'} = \SI{68.5}{\giga\electronvolt}$. 
    The max \gls{DP} mean free path is defined as, 
    \begin{equation}
        \lambda_{max} = \gamma_{max}c\tau_{A'} = \frac{E_{max}}{m_{A'} c^2} c \tau_{A'},
    \end{equation}
    where \gls{DP} mean free path is inversely dependent on $\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2$. The lowest limit from the analysis is found as 
    $\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2 = 3.36 \times 10^{-6}$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt^2\per\clight^4}  %$\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2 = 6.80 \times 10^{-6} MeV^2 /c^4$ 
    corresponding to a max \gls{DP} mean path length of $1.629$\,m. %$0.81\m$.
    Due to the construction of the 3-track trigger and offline reconstruction, the resolution of a decay vertex position is around 1\,m. With a vertex greater than 1\,m, the reconstruction will create a three-track vertex. This will result in the event being removed due to the requirement of a single three-track vertex during the selection, reducing the acceptance.
    However, this is only true in extreme cases where all the energy is propagated from the kaon to the \gls{DP}. In this extreme case, the 1\,m prompt limit is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:prompt} as the dashed lines. The two lines represent the 1\,m limit for the maximum \gls{DP} energy and 50\,\% of the maximum. The fluctuations 
    of the \gls{UL} on the mixing parameter fall between these two lines, suggesting that the prompt assumption is still valid except in the most extreme cases.
    %Suggesting that the current status of the analysis is consistent with the prompt decay mode. From the max energy of the DP, the point at which the mixing parameter for each mass bin would correspond to a 
    %displaced decay is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}.

    \section{Conclusions}
    The search for \gls{DP} at NA62 has been expanded from the study conducted at NA48/2, utilising the 2022 to 2024 datasets. Two methods for the background estimation have been presented: one utilising \gls{MC} samples and the other using the data.
    The \gls{MC} background estimation method produced uncompetitive results due to the limited size of the samples produced. The data-driven background estimation, however, provided improved results, producing competitive limits for the mixing parameter across the 
    \gls{DP} mass search range. The result covers an area not covered by any other experiment at the time of writing.
