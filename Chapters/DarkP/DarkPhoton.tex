    %% Intro with background on NA48/2
    This chapter summarises the search for the \gls{DP} also denoted $A'$, at NA62, with the data collected in 2022-2024.
    The analysis aims to set limits on the mixing parameter of the \gls{DP} utilising the production of $A'$ through the decay of $\pi^0 \to \gamma A'$ and its subsequent decay into SM particles $A' \to e^+e^-$. The theoretical background for this hypothetical \gls{DP} is described in Section \ref{sec:DarkP_th}. 
    The NA62 experiment, described in Section \ref{sec:na62}, provides a clean sample of $\pi^0$ particles through the decay of \mbox{$K^+ \to \pi^+\pi^0$} with a branching ratio of 20.67\,\%. With the assumption that the \gls{DP} decays promptly, the decay signature is identical to the Dalitz decay 
    $\pi^0_D \to \gamma e^+e^-$. The Dalitz decay represents the background for the \gls{DP} search and determines the experiment's sensitivity.

    \section{\boldmath $\pi^0_D$ \unboldmath Event Selection}
    %The analysis started using the same procedure used in the NA48/2 \cite{2015178} %ADD na48 context
    The analysis strategy for the \gls{DP} search originates from the selection used for the search at NA48/2 \cite{2015178}. The \gls{kpid} decay has the same event signature as the production and decay of the \gls{DP}. The event selection comprises the identification of the $\pi^+$, electron, positron and photon. The selection is set out below:
    \subsection{Vertex Selection}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Require that at least one of the following trigger conditions is satisfied: the Control Trigger (also referred to as the Minimum Bias Trigger), the Multi-Track (MT) trigger, or the Electron Multi-Track (eMT) trigger, described in Chapter~\ref{sec:na62}.
        \item A three-track vertex is selected if the total momentum is below \SI{78}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight}, a total positive charge of $Q=1$, a $\chi^{2} < 40$ and a vertex position between $\SI{105}{\meter} < Z_{vtx} < \SI{180}{\meter}$.
        \item The tracks within the vertex are checked if they are in acceptance of all four Straw spectrometer chambers and the CHOD. The track momentum must be $\SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight} < p < \SI{60}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight}$ with a $\chi^2 < 30$. All three tracks must traverse the first STRAW chamber with a separation of 20\,mm from each other.
        \item A selected photon is also required. The missing momentum of the vertex is the difference between the kaon 4-momentum and the total vertex 4-momentum. The momentum is used to extrapolate a straight line from the vertex position to the z-plane of the LKr calorimeter. Within a radius of 15\,cm of the extrapolated position, a single \gls{LKr} energy deposit
        with a minimum energy of \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt} is selected as a photon from the decay. The photon energy must be within \SI{5}{\giga\electronvolt} of the calculated missing vertex momentum.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsection{Kinematic PID}    
    The presence of a three-track vertex is required with a positively charged pion ($\pi^+$) and an electron-positron pair ($e^{\pm}$). The pion is identified from the two positively charged tracks using the missing mass between the kaon 4-momentum and the track 4-momentum, 
    \begin{equation}
        M_{miss}^2 = (P_{K^+} - P_{\pi^+})^2
    \end{equation}
    with $P_{K^+}$ and $P_{\pi^+}$ being the 4-momenta of the kaon and the positive track, respectively.
    If the missing mass of the kaon and the positive track is consistent with the $\pi^0$ mass hypothesis, the track is identified as a charged pion. 
    The cut on the reconstructed missing mass of the kaon and charged pion system is calculated using a \gls{kpid} \gls{MC} sample and is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:kaonpion}.
    The width of the distribution is found to be,
    \begin{equation}
        %\sigma = 3.984 \pm 0.007_{Stat}\, \SI{}{\mega\electronvolt}.
        \sigma = (10.30 \pm 1.43)\,\SI{}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}.
    \end{equation}

    The electron is matched to the negative track. The positive track which does not pass the charged pion selection is identified as the positron.

    The equation used for the elliptical cut in the selection is defined as
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:elipse}
        \frac{((x - x_c)\cos{\theta} + (y-y_c)\sin{\theta})^2}{a^2} + \frac{((y - y_c)\cos{\theta} - (x-x_c)\sin{\theta})^2}{b^2} \leq 1
    \end{equation}
    where $x_c$ and $y_c$ are the ellipse centre. The angle $\theta$ is the rotation of the ellipse, and $a$ and $b$ are the lengths of the semi-major and the semi-minor axis, respectively. 
    The $x$ and $y$ are the reconstructed mass values of the candidate event.
    The parameters are selected to produce a selection efficiency of 95\,\%. The parameters for the ellipse are defined below: 
    \begin{itemize}
        \item $x_c = \SI{493.677}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}$: the x-coordinate of the ellipse centre.
        \item $y_c = \SI{134.977}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}$: the y-coordinate of the ellipse centre.
        \item $a = \SI{40}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}$: the semi-major axis of the ellipse.
        \item $b = \SI{6}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}$: the semi-minor axis of the ellipse.
        \item $\theta = \SI{0.4887}{\radian}$: The rotation angle of the ellipse in radians.
    \end{itemize}

    The difference between the selection used for the NA62 and the NA48/2 study is the \gls{PID}. The study at NA48/2 uses calorimetric \gls{PID} where the \gls{LKr} is utilised to reconstruct the energy of the pion, electron and positron. The reconstructed energy to momentum ratio is used to distinguish 
    between the electron/positron and pion. In the NA62 study, \gls{PID} uses the track momentum measurements to reconstruct the invariant masses, which are compared against the nominal PDG masses \cite{Olive:2014pdg}. This approach is driven by the difference in experimental setup between NA48/2 and NA62. The NA62 setup is longer, as it requires enhanced \gls{PID}, satisfied by the \gls{RICH} and improved momentum measurements provided by the longer spectrometer.
    This results in the \gls{LKr} placed further from the decay volume, reducing the acceptance of the \gls{kpid} decay at NA62 compared to NA48/2. The \gls{LKr} is used in the analysis to reconstruct the photon energy.%, which is required to prevent other backgrounds from passing the selection. 
    
    The impact of the different experimental setups is apparent at low momenta where NA48/2 has an acceptance advantage.

    \section{\gls{MC} Background Estimation Method}
    The \gls{MC} background method only uses the 2022 NA62 dataset. 
    As described above, the background for the search for \gls{DP} is \gls{kpid} and \gls{kmu3D}. These decays are modelled by \gls{MC} and compared against the data to determine the sensitivity of the search. The \gls{MC} samples are produced using the NA62 framework, which simulates the full detector response and the trigger system. The \gls{kpid} and \gls{kmu3D} samples are produced with $2\times10^8$ and $5\times10^6$ events, respectively.
    The limited size of the \gls{MC} samples impact the final result when used for the background estimate. However, due to computing time, it is not feasible to increase the statistics on the samples to improve the final result. Utilising a feature of the NA62 framework, the statistics across a portion of the reconstructed $M_{ee}$ range are increased.
    The true mass of the electron-positron pair is used to calculate the kinematic variable $x$,
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:XParam}
        x = \frac{(Q_{e^-} + Q_{e^+})^2}{M_{\pi^0}^2} = \frac{M_{ee}^2}{M_{\pi^0}^2},
    \end{equation}
    where $Q_{e^-}$ and $Q_{e^+}$ are the four-momenta of the electron and positron, and $M_{\pi^0}$ is the mass of the neutral pion.
    The resultant distribution in terms of the kinematic variable $x$ is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:trueMC_X}. The NA62 software framework allows for the simulation of the Dalitz decay only when the kinematic variable $x > 0.5$. From the true \gls{MC} distribution in 
    Figure~\ref{fig:trueMC_X} the fraction of events where $x > 0.5$ is $3.46\times10^{-3}$ Producing a simulation with the cut on the kinematic variable $x$ with a fraction of the size of the larger sample decreases the statistical errors on the \gls{MC}.

    \subsection{The $\pi^0$ Transition Form Factor}
    The simulation of the $\pi^0_D$ uses the lowest-order differential decay rate \cite{PhysRevD.5.1763},
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:partDecay}
        \frac{d^2\Gamma}{dxdy} = \Gamma_0 \frac{\alpha}{\pi}|F(x)|^2\frac{(1-x)^3}{4x} \left(1+y^2+\frac{r^2}{x}\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $\Gamma_0$ is the $\pi^0 \to \gamma \gamma$ decay rate, $\alpha$ is the fine-structure constant, $F(x)$ is the pion \gls{TFF}, $x$ and $y$ are dimensionless kinematic variables,
    \begin{equation}
        x = \frac{(Q_{e^-} + Q_{e^+})^2}{M_{\pi^0}^2} \quad \text{and} \quad y = \frac{2Q_{\pi^+}(Q_{e^-} - Q_{e^+})}{M_{\pi^0}^2 (1-x)}.
    \end{equation}
    The variables $Q_{e^-}$, $Q_{e^+}$ and $Q_{\pi^+}$ are the four-momenta of the electron, positron and charged pion. Within the kinematic region of Dalitz decay, the \gls{TFF} is approximated as $F(x) = 1 + ax$.     The Vector Meson Dominance (VMD) model expects that \mbox{$a = M_{\pi^0} (M_{\rho}^{-2} + M_{\omega}^{-2}) / 2 = 0.03$ \cite{PhysRevD.83.037503}}.
    The \gls{PDG} average for all experiments is \mbox{$a = 0.0335 \pm 0.0031$} \cite{Tanabashi:2018oca}. It is not possible to rely on external values for the slope factor for the MC due to unknown biases within the simulation.  
    In the NA62 framework, the slope is defined as $a_{Fw} =  0.032$ by default.

The ratio shows a slight discrepancy between the expected background and data. The dependence along $x$ suggests that the TFF found in the framework is incompatible with the background estimate for this data set.

    \subsection{Upper Limit on the Mixing Parameter}
    For each \gls{DP} mass value, the number of data events ($N_{obs}$) is compared to the number of expected events obtained from \gls{MC} ($N_{exp}$), which is scaled by factor $s$, which is calculated from $N_K$. The $N_{obs}$, $N_{exp}$ and their subsequent errors are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:searchwindow_vals}. The error on the observed events $\delta N_{obs}$, which is used for the significance calculation is defined as 
    $\delta N_{obs} = \sqrt{N_{obs}}$. The error on the number of expected events is due to the statistical error of \gls{MC} events within the search window.  A correction is required due to the scale factor imposed on the \gls{MC} to equate for the correct effective kaon flux, defined in Section \ref{sec:kaonFlux}. The error for $N_{exp}$ is defined as 
    $\delta N_{exp} = s \times \sqrt{N_{exp} / s}$.
    \begin{figure}[t]
        \centering 
        \includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{plots/searchwindow_val.png}
        \linespread{1.0}
        \caption{Number of observed data events ($N_{obs}$), expected number of events from \gls{MC} ($N_{exp}$), the respective errors ($\delta N_{obs}$ and $\delta N_{exp}$) 
        and the resultant upper limit on the number of dark photon candidates to a 90\% confidence level.}
        \label{fig:searchwindow_vals}
    \end{figure}
    For each \gls{DP} mass window, the local significance of the \gls{DP} signal is calculated as
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:localSig}
        Z = \frac{N_{obs} - N_{exp}}{\sqrt{\delta N_{obs}^2 + \delta N_{exp}^2}}
    \end{equation}
    and is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:local_sig}. The local significance is never greater than $3 \sigma$, resulting in no \gls{DP} signal being observed. 
    The \gls{UL} for the number of \gls{DP} decays ($A' \to e^+e^-$) at a 90\% \gls{CL} for each \gls{DP} mass value is calculated with $N_{obs}$, $N_{exp}$ and $\delta N_{exp}$ using the CLs method. This method is recommended by the Particle Data Group \cite{Cowan:2018} and employs the process set out in \cite{Junk:1999kv, Read:2000ru}.
    For setting an \gls{UL} on the signal $s$ from a counting experiment where $N$ events are observed ($N_{obs}$), and there is a known expected background $b$ ($N_{exp}$), it is considered a hypothesis test between the two cases: signal+background and just background. According to the Neyman-Pearson lemma \cite{NeymanPearson1933}, the most powerful test statistic for the hypothesis is the likelihood ratio, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:test_stat}
        X = L_{s+b} / L_b = \frac{e^{-s+b}(s+b)^N}{N!} / \frac{e^{-b} b^N}{N!} = e^{-s}(1+\frac{s}{b})^N.
    \end{equation} 
    It is beneficial to denote the test statistic for the observed data as $X_{obs}$.
    A confidence level for the exclusion of the background hypothesis ($\text{CL}_{s+b}$) is the probability that the test statistic defined in Equation~\ref{eq:test_stat} is less than or equal to the observed test statistic, 
    \begin{equation}\label{eq:prob_test}
        CL_{s+b} = P_{s+b}(X \leq X_{obs}).
    \end{equation}
    To improve the computational efficiency, the test statistic defined in Equation~\ref{eq:test_stat} used for comparison is simplified to, 
    \begin{equation}
        log(X) = N log(\frac{s}{b}).
    \end{equation}
    The \gls{CL} is calculated using a \gls{MC} integration method where a large number of trials are used to calculate the probability defined in Equation~\ref{eq:prob_test}. For each trial, the value N is sampled from a Poisson distribution with a mean of $s+b$ where b is the background sampled from a normal distribution with a mean equal to the expected number of events and sigma equal to the error on the expected value.
    The test statistic is computed and compared with $X_{obs}$. Similarly, the confidence level is also calculated for the exclusion of the signal+background ($\text{CL}_{b}$) is calculated, with the difference being that N is sampled from a Poisson distribution with a mean of $b$ instead of $s+b$. 
    After the trials the modified frequentist confidence level ($\text{CL}_s$) is calculated as,
    \begin{equation}
        CL_s = CL_{s+b} / CL_b.
    \end{equation}
    A binary search is then completed using the above method to find the value of $s$, which satisfies the relation, 
    \begin{equation}
        CL_s(s) = 1 - \alpha
    \end{equation}
    which would be the \gls{UL} on the signal for a confidence level of $\alpha$.
    The resultant \gls{UL} on the number of \gls{DP} candidates ($N_{DP}$) at a 90\% \gls{CL} is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:searchwindow_vals}.

    \subsection{Fitting the Data Distribution}
    The method described in Section~\ref{sec:dp_method} is used to convert bins of $M_{ee}$ to \gls{DP} candidate windows.
    The \gls{DP} mass range is defined as $6 \leq M_{A'} \leq 121.6$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}. The mass ranges is extended compared to the previous \gls{MC} study. This is due to the large number of events in the mass bins at the edges of the distribution afforded by only using the data sample rather than the MC. 
    %Each \gls{DP} mass hypothesis is defined by steps of $1\sigma_m(M_{A'})$
    Using the method defined in Section~\ref{sec:dp_method} results in 428 \gls{DP} mass hypotheses tested. 
    %This deviated from the \gls{MC} study where the window's width is the size of the mass centre step. 
    %This is  compared to the \gls{MC} study. %How many?
    A 4th-order polynomial is fitted to the data distribution using a least $\chi^2$ method. The fitting region for each mass hypothesis is defined as $\pm 15\sigma_m(M_{A'})$ from the mass hypothesis, and the distribution is re-binned with a bin width of $\sigma_m(M_{A'}) / 5$. 
    Data events in the mass bins above \SI{125}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared} are removed. This is because the small counts at the edge of the mass range cause instability with the fitting.
    Events in the mass bins $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(M_{A'})$
    of the mass hypothesis are excluded from the fit as the possible presence of a \gls{DP} signal would result in a biased background estimate.
    The number of expected events $N_{exp}$ is evaluated as the integral of the fitted functions across the $\pm 1.5 \sigma_m(M_{A'})$ mass window divided by the bin width. The error on the number of expected events $\delta N_{exp}$ combines statistical and systematic uncertainties. The statistical component $\delta N_{exp}^{stat}$ is computed from the propagation of the 
    statistical errors of the polynomial parameters, provided in the form of a covariance matrix and calculated using an error propagation method available in the ROOT package \cite{Brun1997} (TF1::IntegralError()). 
    The systematic error $\delta N_{exp}^{sys}$ is estimated by fitting each distribution defined above with alternative polynomials, in this case, 3rd and 5th order. The resultant integrals are evaluated and compared to the integral calculated using the main polynomial to produce the systematic error.

    \section{Prompt Limit Calculation}\label{sec:PromptLim}
    The lowest limit on the mixing parameter $\epsilon^2$ occurs at low \gls{DP} mass due to the limited kinematic suppression of the \gls{DP} decay. Key to this analysis is the assumption of the prompt $A' \to e^+e^-$ decay, where \mathcal{B}($A' \to e^+e^-$) = 1. A check on the \gls{DP} maximum mean path length is required to ensure that the above assumption holds and the calculated limits above are correct. 
    %Using the maximum energy of the \gls{DP}, the maximum mean path of the \gls{DP} is calculated. From a nominal beam energy at NA62 of \SI{75}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight}
    The maximum energy of the $\pi^0$ from the $K_{2\pi}$ decay is calculated in the kaons rest reference frame,
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:2body}
        E_{\pi^0}^{rest} = \frac{M^2 + M_{\pi^0}^2 - M_{\pi^+}^2}{2M} = \SI{0.246}{\giga\electronvolt}
    \end{equation}
    where $M$, $M_{\pi^0}$ and $M_{\pi^+}$ are the kaon, neutral pion and charged pion masses, with the equation derived from basic two-body decay kinematics. The $\pi^0$ momentum in the kaon rest frame is defined as
    \begin{equation} \label{eq:pi0mom}
        p_{\pi^0}^{rest} = \sqrt{(E_{\pi^0}^{rest})^2 - M_{\pi^0}^2} = \SI{0.205}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight}.
    \end{equation}
    From a nominal beam energy at NA62 of $E_{K} = \SI{75}{\giga\electronvolt\per\clight}$ and the Equations \ref{eq:2body} and \ref{eq:pi0mom} the maximum energy of the $\pi^0$ in the laboratory reference frame via a Lorentz boost is,
    \begin{equation}
        E^{lab}_{\pi^0} = \gamma_K (E_{\pi^0}^{rest} + \beta p_{\pi^0}^{rest}) = \SI{68.5}{\giga\electronvolt},
    \end{equation}
    with $\gamma_K = E_{K} / M_K$. In the decay $\pi^0 \to \gamma A'$ the maximum energy of the \gls{DP} occurs when all the energy of the neutral pion is transferred to the \gls{DP} and the photon is emitted backwards resulting in a max \gls{DP} energy of $E^{lab}_{A'} = \SI{68.5}{\giga\electronvolt}$. 
    The max \gls{DP} mean free path is defined as, 
    \begin{equation}
        \lambda_{max} = \gamma_{max}c\tau_{A'} = \frac{E_{max}}{M_{A'} c} \tau_{A'} =  0.054 \times \frac{10^{-6}}{\epsilon^2} \times \frac{\SI{100}{\mega\electronvolt\per\clight\squared}}{M_{A'}^2}
    \end{equation}
    where \gls{DP} mean free path is inversely dependent on $\epsilon^2 M_{A'}^2$ and $\tau_{A'}$ is defined by Equation~\ref{eq:DP_lifetime}. The lowest limit from the analysis is found as 
    $\epsilon^2 M_{A'}^2 = 4.59 \times 10^{-6}$\,\si{\mega\electronvolt^2\per\clight^4}  %$\epsilon^2 m_{A'}^2 = 6.80 \times 10^{-6} MeV^2 /c^4$ 
    corresponding to $\lambda_{max} = \SI{1.19}{\meter}$. %$0.81\m$.
    Due to the construction of the 3-track trigger and offline reconstruction, the resolution of a decay vertex position is around 1\,m. With a vertex greater than 1\,m, the probability that a three-track vertex is not produced increases. This results in the event being removed due to the requirement of a single three-track vertex during the selection, reducing the acceptance.
    However, this is only true in extreme cases the \gls{DP} has the maximum energy calculated above. In this extreme case, the $\lambda = \SI{1}{\meter}$ is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:prompt} as the dashed lines for the maximum \gls{DP} energy and 50\,\% of the maximum. The fluctuations 
    of the \gls{UL} on the mixing parameter fall between these two lines, suggesting that the prompt assumption is still valid except in the most extreme cases.

    \section{Conclusions}
    The search for \gls{DP} at NA62 is expanded from the study conducted at NA48/2, utilising the 2022--2024 dataset. Two methods for the background estimation are presented: one utilising \gls{MC} samples and the other using the data.
    The \gls{MC} background estimation method produces uncompetitive results due to the limited size of \gls{MC} samples produced. The data-driven background estimation, however, provides improved results, producing competitive limits for the mixing parameter across the 
    \gls{DP} mass search range. The result sets limits in an area of phase space not yet covered by any other experiment.
