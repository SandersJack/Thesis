    The understanding of the fundamental components and interactions of the universe has cumulated into the formation of the \gls{SM}. The \gls{SM} stands out as one of the most successful models nature produced by science. However, it does not encapsulate all the observations of the universe, such as neutrino masses, dark matter, matter-antimatter asymmetry and the fourth fundamental force of gravity.
    
    Most modern particle physics experiments aim to test the \gls{SM} as precisely as possible and to search for new physics \gls{BSM}. Two experimental methods are employed to reach these aims. One is to reach ever-increasing energies to produce and observe new heavier particles. An example of this method is the experiments at the Large Hadron Collider at CERN, such as ATLAS and CMS, with the successful discovery of 
    the Higgs boson in 2012. The second method involves collecting large data sets to search for rare processes where any deviations from the \gls{SM} can provide evidence of \gls{BSM} physics. An example of this method is the search for $K^+ \to \pi \nu \bar\nu$ by NA62 at CERNs \gls{SPS} with its goal to measure the branching ratio, which is precisely predicted by the \gls{SM}. 

    A more detailed description of the \gls{SM} and the involvement of kaon physics can be found in Chapter \ref{sec:theory}. 

    The work completed within this thesis was undertaken within the NA62 collaboration. This thesis includes the theoretical foundations for the work completed throughout, as found in Chapter \ref{sec:theory}. Chapter \ref{sec:na62} describes the NA62 experimental setup and the latest results published by the collaboration for the search for $K^+ \to \pi \nu \bar\nu$.
    Chapter \ref{sec:cedarH} describes the development of a new CEDAR with a hydrogen radiator gas for use within the NA62 kaon identification system. An analysis project for the search for the \gls{DP} at NA62 is presented in Chapter \ref{sec:darkP}, where two methods are presented. Finally, the work completed for the \gls{HIKE} proposal is presented in Chapter \ref{chap:FutureKaon} along with the upgrades to the NA62 offline software to facilitate simulations of new experimental setups.
