    Over the past 75\,years the study of kaon physics has been integral to the development of the standard model. Since 2016, the NA62 experiment has been the leading experiment in the field of kaon physics. However, the future of kaon physics is uncertain
    , with the NA62 experiment funded up to CERN LS3, which is expected at the end of 2025. It was then required to investigate the future of kaon physics and the potential experiments that could succeed NA62. This chapter will discuss the potential future of kaon physics, the experiments that could be used to further the field, and the 
    work contributed to these experiments.
    \section{High Intensity Kaon Experiments (HIKE) Description}
    The High-intensity kaon experiments (HIKE) was a proposed project to continue kaon physics at CERN in the ECN3 experimental hall and represented a long-phased plan after LS3. HIKE would of profited from a four to six-fold increase in beam intensity compared to NA62 and included cutting-edge detector technologies that will be desicred in this 
    section. There were three phases within the HIKE program, the first being a continuation of NA62's charged kaon physics with the high-intensity beam and detector upgrades. The second would have involved neural kaon physics with charged secondary particle tracking, and the third phase would have been designed to measure the $K_{L} \to \pi^{0}\nu\bar{\nu}$ decay. 
    \subsection{Phase 1}
    The experimental design of NA62 has been proven as a successful setup for the precision measurement of the $K^{+} \to \pi^{+}\nu\bar{\nu}$ decay. The HIKE phase 1 detector setup is shown in figure \ref{fig:Phase1} with new and upgraded detectors with respect to the NA62 setup described in Section \ref{sec:na62}. The new and upgraded detectors are required due to the 
    four times greater particle rate with respect to NA62 equating to $1.2\, \times 10^{13}$ protons per spill on the target. Other than the intensity upgrade, the beam had similar characteristics to NA62 with a $75\,Gev / c$ secondary beam produced from the $400\,GeV / c$ primary beam from the SPS accelerator onto a target at an angle of $0\,rad$. 
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.99\textwidth]{imgs/Phase1.png}
        \caption{Diagram of HIKE phase 1 detector setup \cite{thehikecollaboration2023high}}%
        \label{fig:Phase1}%
    \end{figure}  
    The upgraded detectors before the fiducial volume included an upgraded photon-detection system for the KTAG, described in section \ref{sec:ktag_upgrade} and a new GTk chip design described in section \ref{sec:gtk_upgrade}. It was proposed to upgrade the existing NA62 Vetocounter with scintillating fiber (SciFi) technologies to improve the granularity 
    and to accommodate the increased beam intensity. 

    Major upgrades were envisioned to the LAV and STRAW detectors within the fiducial volume. Some of the LAV stations would have been upgraded to a new design proposed for a lead scintillator design, similar to the Vacuum Veto System (VVS) planned to be used at the CKM experiment. The LAV detectors were important for the next phases of HIKE, especially for 
    Phase 3 and would have been designed for the requirements of the $K_{L} \to \pi^{0}\nu\bar{\nu}$ measurement even when used in this Phase. The STRAW spectrometer would have been upgraded to use a new 5\,mm straw design shown in Figure \ref{fig:5mmstraw}. The reduction in straw diameter would have decreased drift times and increase granularity resulting in an 
    expected improvement in the track angle and momentum resolution of 10-20\,\% compared to NA62.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{imgs/5mm_straw.png}
        \caption{Diagram of the expected layout of the 5\,mm straws \cite{thehikecollaboration2023high}}%
        \label{fig:5mmstraw}%
    \end{figure} 
    Downstream of the fiducial volume, the PMT arrays of the RICH detector would have been upgraded, which is described in Section \ref{sec:rich_upgrade}. The timing detector would profit from the current NA62-CHOD detector with upgraded PMTs, which would have been chosen from the number of 
    studies from other detectors such as KTAG, RICH and MUV. For Phase 1, the primary electromagnetic calorimeter would have used the LKr from NA62, which had excellent energy resolution but was not satisfactory timing. The required timing resolution would have been achieved by upgrading 
    the readout system. A number of other detectors would have been upgraded or replaced with higher granularity, such as the hadronic calorimeter, MUVs, IRC, and SAC.
    \subsection{Phase 2}
    HIKE phase 2 would have been a multi-purpose neutral $K_{L}$ decay experiment with the primary goal to measure the branching ratios of the $K_{L} \to \pi^{0} l^{+} l^{-}$ decays. The detector setup for phase 2, shown in Figure \ref{fig:Phase2} would have involved minimal modification to the experimental 
    setup of Phase 1, with the removal of detectors that were only applicable to the charge kaon beam. The KTAG, GTk, RICH, and SAC detectors would have been removed the straw spectrometer shortened to account for the softer momentum of the secondary particles, and the chambers would have been reconfigured to have 
    central beam holes to account for the neural beam. Due to the compact detector, the fiducial volume would have been increased to 90\,m compared to 75\,m in Phase 1.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.99\textwidth]{imgs/Phase2.png}
        \caption{Diagram of HIKE phase 2 detector setup \cite{thehikecollaboration2023high}}%
        \label{fig:Phase2}%
    \end{figure}  
    The design proposed a production angle of 2.4\,mrad on the target producing an expected $K_{L}$ yield of $5.4 \times 10^{-5}$ per proton. Assuming an integrated proton flux of $1.2 \times 10^{19}$ pot per year would have resulted in $3.8 \times 10^{13}$ $K_{L}$ decays in the fiducial volume per year. The mean momentum at the entry of the fiducial volume was expected to be 
    $79\,GeV/c$ with a mean momentum for $K_{L}$ which decay of $46\,GeV/c$. 
    \subsection{Phase 3 (KLEVER)}
    HIKE phase 3, previously known as KLEVER \cite{ambrosino2019klever}, was an experiment designed to measure the $K_{L} \to \pi^{0}\nu\bar{\nu}$ decay. The experimental setup is shown in Figure \ref{fig:Phase3} and differs from the design used at NA62 and HIKE Phase1/2. The setup consists of a large number of photon-detectors, located around a 160\,m long vacuum volume to produce a complete 
    coverage of photons produced from $K_{L}$ decays. The photon-detectors include coverage for photons emitted up to 100\,mrad and allow for a free path of photons to the main electromagnetic calorimeter (MEC) emitted up to 7.5\,mrad. 
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.99\textwidth]{imgs/Phase3.png}
        \caption{Diagram of HIKE phase 3 detector setup \cite{thehikecollaboration2023high}}%
        \label{fig:Phase3}%
    \end{figure}  
    Due to the differing experimental setup, Phase 3 was separated from the HIKE proposal submitted in 2023. Still, some detectors would have been shared between the two experiments as the granularity and efficiency requirements for Phase 3 would have driven the detector requirements for the first two phases. The LAV's, MEC, IRC and SAC and the hadronic calorimters designed an built for HIKE Phase 1 and 2 would of been 
    used in Phase 3. The upstream veto counter, charged particle veto, and the pre-shower detector are unique to Phase 3. 
    \section{KTAG Photon Detection System upgrade} \label{sec:ktag_upgrade}
    To sustain the increased kaon rate, the KTAG required upgrades to the photon-detection system and data acquisition system, with an expected time resolution of 15 - 20\,ps and a kaon identification efficiency of greater than 95\,\%. The expected kaon rate was 200\,MHz which corresponds to a maximum particle rate $10\,MHz/cm^{2}$ of photons at the PMT arrays, taking into the 
    production of Cherenkov photons in CEDAR-H and the resultant acceptance of the photons. It was proposed that the PMT arrays would be replaced with arrays of micro-channel plate photomultipliers (MCP-PMTs), which provide a single photon time resolution of 50 - 70\,ps depending on the model, a low dark rate of $1\,kHz/cm^{2}$ and the ability to sustain the required photon rate.
    A downside of the MCP-PMTs is their limited lifetime from the aging of the Photo-Cathode caused by heavy feedback ions from the residual gas. This results in the rapid degradation of PMT quantum efficiency with the increasing integrated anode charge.

    \section{GTk upgrade design} \label{sec:gtk_upgrade}
    The NA62 GTk described in section \ref{sec:GTK} would have required an upgrade to sustain the fourfold increase in intensity at HIKE, requiring the new detector to have a time resolution of 50\,ps compared to 200\,ps in NA62. The exact pixel technology was not specified and is not part of this study, with the upgrade being focused on the improved granularity. 
    The current NA62 GTk has two different pixel sizes: small, which are 300x300x200$\mu m$, and large, which are 400x150x200$\mu m$. The large pixels are found on the edge of the 10 chips that make up each station, and the small pixels are found in the center. Two options were envisioned to fulfill requirements for HIKE, the first having a pixel size on half of NA62 and the second having a pixel size of a third of NA62.
    The NA62MC simulation and NA62 reconstruction software were upgraded to allow for a more flexible GTk design. 

    For the HIKE simulations, the pixel size of one-third of NA62 was chosen to maximize the granularity of the GTk, with the small pixel size being 150x150x200$\mu m$ and the larger pixel size being 200x150x200$\mu m$.

    \section{RICH upgrade design} \label{sec:rich_upgrade}
    The overall design of the NA62 RICH described in section \ref{sec:RICH} was well suited for use within HIKE with an upgrade to the Cherenkov photon-detection system. The NA62 RICH photon detection system comprises two arrays on either side of the beam pipe, equipped with Hamamatsu R7400-U03 PMTs placed in a pattern described in Figure \ref{fig:richPMTArray}. 
    The PMTs have a single hit time resolution of 240\,ps and a quantum efficiency of 20\,\%. Due to the placement of the PMTs, the distance between the center of each PMT is 18\,mm, which is constrained by the sensor size and the geometry of PMT itself. This distance between sensors was the main contributor to the single hit position resolution of 4.7\,mm, 
    resulting in an overall ring radius resolution of 1.5\,mm. The number of hits per ring candidate and the single hit time resolution produced a time resolution of 80\,ps. Due to the increased beam intensity, the main requirement of the RICH for HIKE was a time resolution of 20 - 30\,ps.
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{plots/diagramRICH.png}
        \caption{Diagram of RICH PMT array}%
        \label{fig:richPMTArray}%
    \end{figure}  
    The proposed RICH photon detection system upgrade would replace the current PMTs with a smaller sensor size, allowing for a smaller distance between sensor centers and reducing the hit position resolution. An excellent candidate for a sensor replacement was silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs), with small sensor sizes, high quantum efficiency of 40\,\% and a single hit time resolution 
    of at least 100\,ps were available from major manufacturers. Three sensor sizes were selected for further investigation, $9 \times 9\,mm$, $6 \times 6\,mm$, and $3 \times 3\,mm$. Each size had apparent benefits and downsides, but the smaller sensors would produce a better hit position resolution but at an increased cost and complexity.
    A study was conducted to determine the optimal sensor size for the SiPMs, with the overall detector performance balanced with the cost and complexity. 

    Using the upgraded NA62 software package described in section \ref{sec:na62fw}, it was possible to implement the new PMTs into the RICH Monte Carlo simulation with the implemented arrays shown in Figure \ref{fig:RICH_PMTs}. Compared to the arrays currently used in NA62, the PMTs have been aligned on the vertical axis, and because of the geometry of the new PMTs, the are of the light spot, shown in Figure \ref{fig:RICH_PMTs} as a grey circle, is completely covered by the PMTs with 
    no gaps between the sensors.

    The letter of intent for HIKE proposed a SiPM with a Quantum efficiency of at least 40\,\% \cite{CortinaGil:2839661}. The Hamamatsu S13360-**50CS series was selected as the preliminary candidate for the RICH PMT upgrade to achieve this requirement. Hamamatsu offers a number of SiPMs in the S13360 series, with the **50CS selected for its improved Ultra Violet (UV) sensitivity and a high quantum efficiency of 40\,\% peak at 460\,nm, 
    shown in Figure \ref{fig:RICH_PMT_QE}. 
    \begin{figure}[H]
        \centering
        \includegraphics[width=0.6\textwidth]{plots/PMT_QE.png}
        \caption{Selected SiPM quantum efficiency taken from Hamamatsu S13360 datasheet \cite{MPPC}}%
        \label{fig:RICH_PMT_QE}%
    \end{figure}  
    Along with the QE efficiency requirement was a single hit time resolution of 100\,ps \cite{CortinaGil:2839661}. The manufacturer stated the Hamamatsu S13360-**50CS series has excellent time resolution, but no definite value was given. The time resolution would also depend on the final design of the RICH readout electronics and would have to be measured in a test setup. A simple Gaussian time response curve with a sigma of 100\,ps was used for the simulations.
    
    The expected performance of the RICH detector with the new PMTs was compared to the current NA62 RICH detector in Table \ref{tab:hikeRICH}. With the increased quantum efficiency, the expected number hits per $\pi^{+}$ at 15 and 45\,$GeV/c$ increased by a factor of two. This, along with the improved time resolution of the new PMTs resulted in a time $\pi^+$ time resolution of 27\,ps at 15\,$GeV/c$ and 20\,ps at 45\,$GeV/c$.

    The time resolution of the RICH detector was calculated using the Ke3 sample by equally splitting the reconstructed hits of the ring candidate into two sets and calculating the time difference between the two sets. The results are shown in Figure \ref{fig:NA62RICHTIMERes}, with the 3x3\,mm sensors producing the best time resolution of 20.04\,ps compared to the 9x9\,mm sensors with a time resolution of 21.22\,ps.
    Each sensor size produced a time resolution of less than 30\,ps, fulfilling the requirements of HIKE. 
    
    The 9x9\,mm sensor size was selected for future use in the HIKE RICH detector. This size was chosen as it fulfills the HIKE requirements of time resolution without the added complexity and cost of the smaller sensors, the 9x9 array has a total of 8588 sensors on each of the Salev and Jura sides of the RICH detector compared to 72780 for the 3x3\,mm.
