
The \gls{ch} was designed by The University of Birmingham and built at CERN where it was characterised and tested at a test-beam in 2022. 
\gls{ch} was installed and commissioned in the NA62 beam-line for the 2023 run where a number of pressure scans and other tests were completed to ensure that the detector was ready for data taking. 
The detector produced excellent results with 13\,\% greater light yield compared with \gls{cw} with \gls{n2}. \gls{ch} has been in operation since 2023 and has perform flawlessly through out.

The search for \gls{DP} at NA62 was conducted with two studies with different background estimation methods: MC and data-driven. The MC study did not produce competitive results when compared to previous experiments in the 
search region so the data-driven background method was used. Utilising the data-driven method the \gls{UL} of the mixing parameter squared was set and provides coverage across a region which has not been covered by any other experiments.
It is planned that this search will continue by implementing data collected in the two final years of data taking at NA62 to further enhance the result.

The NA62 software package was overhauled to allow for more flexibility. Thi allowed for the implementation and testing of new detectors for future experiments such as \gls{HIKE}. The upgrades planned for the KTAG and RICH were presented for use within \gls{HIKE} Phase 1 and the 
detector rates for the expected positions for \gls{HIKE} Phase 2 were presented. The results from the simulations were used in the \gls{HIKE} proposal submitted in 2023 to the SPS committee. Unfortunately the \gls{HIKE} experiment was not approved. However, the upgraded software and studies conducted were utilised for the KOTO II proposal submitted in 2024.
