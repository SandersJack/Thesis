Any deviations from theoretical understanding could be hint of physics BSM. 1.2 Kaon Physics The first possible observation a heavy meson, a particle composed of a quark- antiquark pair, was in by Leprince-Ringuet and L’heritier [9, 10] and predated even the discovery of the pion in [11]. Utilising a cloud chamber to measure cosmic rays they found the first evidence of a particle with a mass of times the mass of the electron (0 .511 MeV [12]). However, it was possible that the upper limit on the measured mass could reach the proton mass so they were not credited with the discovery. In while similarly studying cosmic rays with cloud chambers G. D. Rochester and C. C. Butler published a result which included two pictures showing forked tracks [13]. One of the tracks proved to show the decay of a neutral particle into two charged daughter particles, which turned out to be decay of the neutral kaon to two charged pions. The other track showed the decay of a charged particle into charged and neutral daughter particles. This result was later confirmed in by a group in Cal-Tech who observed multiple instances of these decays [14]. However, between these publications a group in Bristol used emulsion chambers to observe the decay of a charged particle into three charged particles [15], known then as the tau decay. This resulted in what was known as the tau-theta problem, where the seemingly the same particle was decaying into two different final states with different parities: Theta to two pions (+1) and Tau to three pions (-1). This eventually led to the discovery that parity is not conserved in weak interactions [16]. In the early 1950s these particles would be described as K-mesons, for which there are four interaction (weak) eigenstates ( K+,K−,K0and ¯K0). Experiments continued to measure the characteristics of the K mesons and found features such as unusually long lifetimes (10−10seconds) despite their rapid production [17]. These features arise from the fact the production of Kmesons occurs through the strong interaction, whereas their decays proceed through the weak interaction. These de- 81.2 Theoretical foundations Kaon Physics viations from the other previously discovered mesons such as the the pion suggested the existence of a new quantum number named strangeness [18, 19]. The strange quantum number applies to all strongly interacting particle and is conserved in strong interactions but violated in weak interactions. Into the latter 1950s and early 1960s the neutral kaon became the center of number of studies because of their relative ease of production and long lifetimes made them easy to study. Despite the fact that parity is violated in weak interactions it was still believed that the combination of CP would be conserved. Through experiment the two mass (strong) eigenstates of the neutral kaon showed to have different lifetimes, which resulted in their names KLfor long and KSfor short [20]. Each of these eigenstates had different CP values with the KSwith CP= +1 decaying to two pions and the KLwith CP=−1 observed decaying to three pions. However, in it was observed that the KLdecayed to two pions [21] proving that the CP symmetry was indeed violated in weak decays. This decay is an example of indirect CP violation as it occurs through neural kaon mixing. The first evidence of direct CP violation, where the violation occurs within the decay was provided by the NA31 experiment in [22]. The process was conclusively observed by KTeV and NA48 collaborations in the latter 1990s [23, 24]. The space between the quadrupoles is occupied by a pair of collimators (C1, C2), which redefines the horizontal and vertical acceptance of the beam. The collimator after the Q6 quadrupole (C3) is employed to absorb positrons that had their momenta degraded by the radiator and again redefines the acceptance in the vertical direc- tion. The beam next passes through a mm field-free hole in a set of iron plates, placed between three m dipole magnets (B3). The vertical magnetic field in the iron plates is used to deflect muons. Any deviation in the beam due to stray fields in the beam hole is cancelled out by a pair of steering dipoles (TRIM2 and TRIM3). Two quadrupoles (Q7, Q8) follow and ensure that the beam is parallel before the KTAG, which comprises of a CEDAR and a purpose-built photon detection system, described in Section 2.3.1. The light produced is reflected back upstream by a Mangin mirror [60]. This is a lens comprising two spherical surfaces, with the downstream surface cemented onto a concave mirror having an identical radius of curvature. The mirror is designed to reduce spherical aberrations, an effect intrinsic to lenses and curved mirrors where the light that strikes the spherical surface off-centre is refracted/reflected to different points compared to the light that would strike the centre of the mirror. After the mirror, the light goes through a plano-convex lens named the Chromatic Corrector, which is designed in such a way that it corrects for the chromatic dispersion of the radiator gas and directs the light towards the diaphragm. Both the Mangin mirror and the Chromatic corrector have a hole in the centre to allow for the beam to pass through without impacting the optics. The analysis aims to set limits on the mixing parameter of the DP utilising the production of A′through the decay of π0→γA′ and its subsequent decay into SM particles A′→e+e−. The theoretical background for this hypothetical DP is described in Section 1.3. The NA62 experiment, described in Section provides a clean sample of π0particles through the decay of K+→ π+π0with a branching ratio of 20.67 %. With the assumption that the DP decays promptly, where the A′decays at the point of its production, the decay signature is identical to the Dalitz decay π0 D→e+e−. This decay represents the background for the DP search and determines the experiment’s sensitivity. 4.1 Event Selection The analysis strategy for the DP search originated from the selection used for the search at NA48/2 [44]. As the K→π0 Dπ+(K2πD) decay has the same event signature as the DP the selection strategy involves the selection of the π+and an electron- positron pair. All three tracks must traverse the first chamber of the straw with a separation of cm from each other. Require the presence of a three-track in the vertex with a selected positively charged pion ( π+) and a selected electron-positron pair ( e±). The pion is selected from the two positively charged tracks using the missing mass between the kaon momentum. The kaon momentum is taken from the average beam momentum. If the missing mass of the kaon and a positive track is within five sigma of the π0mass hypothesis, it is selected as a charged pion. If both positive tracks satisfy the condition, the event is vetoed. From MC, only approximately .1 % of Dalitz decays have both positive tracks satisfying the selection of the charged pion. The electron is assigned to the only negative track. The positive track which doesn’t pass the charged pion selection is selected as the positron. A selected photon is also required. The missing momentum of the vertex is calculated with the kaon momentum and the total vertex momentum. CEDAR-H was in- stalled and commissioned in the NA62 beam-line for the run where a number of pressure scans and other tests were completed to ensure that the detector was ready for data taking. The detector produced excellent results with % greater light yield compared with CEDAR-W with N 2. CEDAR-H has been in operation since and has perform flawlessly through out. The search for DP at NA62 was conducted with two studies with different back- ground estimation methods: MC and data-driven. The MC study did not produce competitive results when compared to previous experiments in the search region so the data-driven background method was used. Utilising the data-driven method the UL of the mixing parameter squared was set and provides coverage across a region which has not been covered by any other experiments. It is planned that this search will continue by implementing data collected in the two final years of data taking at NA62 to further enhance the result. The NA62 software package was overhauled to allow for more flexibility. Thi allowed for the implementation and testing of new detectors for future experiments such as HIKE. The upgrades planned for the KTAG and RICH were presented for use within HIKE Phase and the detector rates for the expected positions for HIKE Phase were presented. The results from the simulations were used in the HIKE proposal submitted in to the SPS committee. Unfortunately the HIKE experiment was not approved.
