3.1 Overview
The NA62 experiment is a fixed target experiment located in the north area of
CERN with a high energy particle beam provided by the Super Proton Synchrotron.
The main goal of the experiment is to measure the branching fraction of the ultra-
rare charged kaon decay K+→π+ν¯νwhich is of the order of 10−10 to a 15%
precision. Alongside there is a expansive physics program of precision and rare
decay measurements as well as searches for forbidden processes and exotic particles.
The experiment employs a decay-in-flight technique to obtain a large number
of charged kaon decays, in the order of 1013within the short time frame of a few
years. The experimental setup was designed to fulfill all the requirements for the
challenging meaurement of K+→π+ν¯ν, which involves the detection of the K+
upstream and the detection of the product π+, with a missing energy-momentum
carried away by the undetected neutrino-antinutrino pair. The measurement of the
missing mass is used for kinematic rejection of the main background K+decays;

3.3.4 Ring Imaging Cherenkov counter (RICH)
The Ring Imaging Cherenkov counter (RICH) was designed to separate secondary
pion and muons within a momentum range of 15 - 35 GeV/c . The ability to sepa-
rate the secondary particles is required as the largest background to K+→π+ν¯ν
comes from the K+→µ+νµ, which has a BR 10 orders of magnitude greater. The
majority of the suppression comes from kinematic selections and the response differ-
ence between pions and muons in the calorimeters, however, another factor of 100
is required in the momentum range 15 - 35 GeV/c was required, which the RICH
satisfied.
The RICH is a 17.5 m long cylindrical vessel comprising four steel sections with
diameters gradually decreasing from 4.2 m at the upstream section to accommodate
the PMT flanges to 3.2 m in the downstream end. The radiator vessel is separated

CEDARH
CEDARs were designed and built at CERN to detect secondary beam particles
at the Super Proton Synchrotron (SPS). Two types of CEDAR were developed at
CERN: CEDAR-W and CEDAR-N, corresponding to the areas of use. The two
designs differ in terms of energy range in which they can detect beam particles,
with CEDAR-N able to separate kaons and pions up to 300 GeV/c but not detect
protons below 60 GeV/c . Whereas CEDAR-W can detect protons down to 12 GeV/c
and separate kaons and pions up to 150 GeV/c .
Described in section , NA62 has run with a CEDAR-W since 2016 filled with
Nitrogen ( at a pressure of 1.71 bar (absolute pressure). In this configuration, the
CEDAR contributed to the most significant amount of material in the path of the
beam, introducing 39 ×10−3X0of material, where X0is one radiation length. The
aluminum windows of the CEDAR vessel contribute 3 .9×10−3X0and the gas
contributes 35 ×10−3X0. The material from the with results in multiple scattering
of beam particles, which introduces an angular divergence of 32 µrad. Scattered
beam particles contribute to the backgrounds for the searches for ultra-rare decays,

Once the inital parameters had been found, a full GEANT4 simulation was
employed to validate the results from the analytical computation. After some more
optimisation, the simulation was then used to modify the optical parameters to
maximise the number of photons reaching the KTAG PMTs. The radius of the beam
hole of the Mangin mirror was reduced with respect to the original CEDAR design,
resulting in a greater reflective surface and increasing the number of photons reaching
the PMTs. The spherical mirrors were updated to improve the light distribution on
the PMT arrays. It was found that using the condenser lenses from the CEDAR-W
compared to the CEDAR-N resulted in more significant photon yield.
During the optimization process, it was found that the results showed negligible
differences between 3.8 and 4.1 bar as the increase of Cherenkov photons at high
pressure was offset by the improved light distribution on the PMT array at low
pressures. Due to safety considerations, the lower pressure of 3.8 bar was selected.

4.3 Test-beam at CERN
Figure 4.13: Image of CEDAR-H being installed into H6 beamline in 2022
After construction of the CEDAR-H, the detector was tested and characterised in
2022 at a test beam at the H6 beam-line at CERN, shown in Figure ??. There
were two main aims of the test beam: to ensure that the optics had been installed
and aligned correctly, to measure the kaon identification probably and the pion
missidentication probability. The H6 beamline is located at CERN’s north area
and is derived from 400 GeV/c primary protons from the SPS interacting with a
target consisting of a beryllium rod, producing an unseparated 75 GeV/c hadron
beam similar to NA62. CEDAR-H was installed 440 m downstream of the target,
compared to 70 m when installed in NA62. Resulting in a slightly different beam
composition when compared to NA62, with 4 % kaons, 25 % protons, and 71 % pions.
The beam divergence at the entrance of the CEDAR was 80 µmin the H6 beamline
compared to 70 µmin NA62, well within the operating divergence of the CEDAR.
In the test beam, an ET 9820QB PMT was installed onto each exit window
and operated at a voltage of 2 kV with a 30 mV discriminator voltage to achieve
